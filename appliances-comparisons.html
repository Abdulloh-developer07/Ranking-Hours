Home Appliance Comparisons and Buying Guide

Washing machines, fridges, air conditioners, vacuums and air purifiers explained in plain words, with links to our full comparisons.

Last updated September 30, 2026

Start here

Home appliances are the things we buy rarely and live with for years. A phone is replaced in three years, but a fridge or a washing machine is expected to run for ten or more. That is why a bad choice hurts: you do not just lose money, you live with the annoyance every day.

This page is the starting point for our home appliance guides. It explains the ideas that matter for almost every appliance, then goes through the main types one by one, and links to our detailed comparisons for washing machines, air conditioners, refrigerators and air purifiers.

A short note on how we work. We base our guides on published specifications, official documents and public information. We do not claim to have tested every appliance, and we do not make up measurements. Models and prices change often, and they differ between sellers, so we do not print price lists. Always check the exact model before you buy.

Five questions before you look at any model

1. How big is the need?

Count the people in your home and how you live. A family of five washes far more clothes than a couple. A large kitchen needs a different fridge from a small flat. Buying too small is annoying. Buying too big wastes electricity and space.

2. Where will it go?

Measure the space, the door, the stairs and the lift. Many appliances are delivered and then do not fit through a narrow hallway. Leave room around the product for air and for opening doors.

3. What does the home provide?

Check the electrical supply, the water pressure, the drain and the ventilation. A good machine in a bad setup will not perform well, and in some cases the warranty may be affected.

4. Who will fix it?

Ask where the service centre is, how quickly parts arrive and what the warranty covers. A cheap appliance with no local service is a risk. A well-known one with a nearby service point is often worth some extra money.

5. What is the total cost?

Think about the price, delivery, installation, electricity, water, filters and repairs over ten years. A product that is cheap to buy but costly to run can end up the most expensive.

Understanding energy and efficiency labels

Many appliances carry an energy label. These labels use letters or stars to show how efficiently the product uses electricity, and they help you compare similar models. A more efficient model usually costs more at the start and less to run.

There are a few things to remember. Labels differ between regions and have changed over the years, so a letter on one label does not match the same letter on another. Compare products using the same label system. The label also shows consumption under standard test conditions, and your own use will differ. Finally, the saving depends on how often you use the product. For an appliance that runs all day, like a fridge, efficiency matters a lot. For one that runs a few hours a week, it matters less.

To estimate running costs, multiply the yearly energy figure, in kilowatt-hours, by your electricity price. This gives you a rough yearly cost that you can compare between models.

Power supply and local conditions

In Uzbekistan the standard household supply is 220 volts at 50 hertz, and many homes use European-style round-pin sockets. Most appliances sold here are made for this supply. Still, check the label on the product for voltage and plug type, especially if you buy an imported model.

Voltage can fluctuate in some areas, and sudden surges or drops can harm motors and electronics. A voltage stabiliser or a surge protector can be a sensible protection for expensive appliances, especially refrigerators, air conditioners and washing machines. Ask an electrician what suits your home.

Summer heat also matters. Fridges and air conditioners work harder in high temperatures. Look for products rated for hot climates, and give them good ventilation.

Water quality is another point. In places with hard water, scale builds up in washing machines, dishwashers and kettles. Look for ways to reduce scale, such as softening filters or regular descaling.

Washing machines

A washing machine looks simple, but there are several choices that change daily life. Our full guide is LG vs Bosch washing machines. Here are the main points.

Capacity

Capacity is given in kilograms of dry laundry. A common family size is around seven or eight kilograms, and couples may be fine with less. Remember that you should not fill the drum completely for the best result.

Spin speed

Spin speed is in revolutions per minute. Higher speeds remove more water, so clothes dry faster, but they are harder on some fabrics and can make the machine louder.

Motor type

Many modern machines use an inverter motor, which is quieter, efficient and often comes with a longer motor warranty. Direct-drive designs remove belts, which can mean fewer wear parts.

Programmes and features

Most people use a handful of programmes. Steam, quick wash and allergy cycles are useful for some, and extras like app control are nice but rarely essential. Pay more attention to the drum quality, door seal, noise level and ease of cleaning.

Front or top loading

Front-loading machines are generally more efficient and gentle on clothes. Top-loading ones are easier on the back and can be narrower, which suits small spaces.

Refrigerators

A fridge runs every hour of every day, so efficiency and reliability matter most. Our comparison of Samsung and Artel refrigerators shows how an international brand and a local one differ.

Size and layout

Capacity is shown in litres, but usable space depends on shelves and shape. Think about how you shop. People who buy a lot of fresh food need a larger fridge section. People who stock frozen food need more freezer space. Side-by-side and French-door models are wide, so check your space.

Climate class

Fridges have a climate class that shows the room temperatures they are designed for. A product made for hot rooms, usually marked ST or T, keeps its cooling in high summer heat. In a hot climate, this is worth checking.

Cooling technology

No-frost systems stop ice building up and save you from defrosting. Inverter compressors adjust their speed, which saves energy and runs quietly. Some models have separate cooling for the fridge and the freezer, which keeps food fresher.

Noise and placement

Fridges sit in the living area in many homes, so noise matters. Leave space at the back and sides for air to circulate, and keep the fridge away from the oven and direct sun.

Air conditioners

In a hot summer, an air conditioner is a necessity. Our guide Gree vs Samsung air conditioners goes into detail. These are the main points.

Cooling power

Power is given in BTU per hour or in kilowatts. As a rough guide, small rooms need less and big rooms need more, but the right size depends on sunlight, insulation, ceiling height, windows and how many people use the room. An undersized unit struggles on hot days, and an oversized one can cycle on and off and feel less comfortable. A professional can calculate the right size.

Inverter or fixed speed

An inverter model adjusts its output, so it holds the temperature steadily, runs quietly and uses less electricity. Fixed-speed models are cheaper to buy but switch on and off, which uses more power. For daily use in summer, inverter models usually pay back the extra cost.

Installation

Installation quality matters as much as the unit. A poor installation causes leaks, noise and loss of efficiency. Use a qualified installer, and ask what the warranty covers. Pipe length, the position of the outdoor unit and a proper drain are all important.

Filters and maintenance

Dust-clogged filters make the unit work harder and the air worse. Clean the filters regularly, and have the unit serviced once a year or as the maker advises.

Vacuum cleaners and robot vacuums

Vacuums have changed a lot. Cordless stick models are popular, and robot vacuums have become common.

For a cordless model, look at suction power, battery life, the weight and the filter. Remember that battery runtime is usually quoted at a low power level, and it falls fast at maximum power. Check whether the battery can be replaced, since it will wear out after some years.

For a corded model, you get steady power and no battery worry, but you have to manage the cable.

Robot vacuums keep floors tidy with little effort. They work best on mostly clear floors, and they need regular emptying of the dust bin and cleaning of brushes. They do not replace a full vacuum for deep cleaning. Look at the mapping, the obstacle detection and how easy it is to maintain.

Think about your floors and your home. Pets and long hair need good brush design. Allergy sufferers should look for sealed systems with good filters.

Air purifiers

Air purifiers filter dust, pollen and some pollutants from indoor air. Our comparison Dyson vs Xiaomi air purifier covers two popular approaches.

Key points: choose a purifier rated for your room size, since a small unit in a big room will not do much. Look at the filter type. A true HEPA filter catches very fine particles. Check the clean air delivery rate, which tells you how much clean air the device produces. Also look at noise at the speeds you will really use, and at the cost and availability of replacement filters, because you will buy them regularly.

A purifier helps most in rooms where windows stay closed. It does not replace ventilation and cleaning, and it does not remove every kind of pollutant. If you have a health condition, ask your doctor about what to use.

Other appliances worth a thought

Dishwashers

They save water and time for families, if you use them fully loaded. Check the number of place settings, the noise and the rack layout. Hard water needs salt or a softener system.

Microwaves and ovens

For a microwave, size and power matter, and a turntable that fits your plates. For an oven, check the capacity, the heating modes and how easy it is to clean. Built-in models need exact measurements.

Water heaters

Choose capacity for your household and check the heating method and safety features. Installation should be done by a professional.

Small kitchen appliances

Kettles, blenders and air fryers are cheap, and you can try them without a big risk. Choose by how often you will use them, and keep the ones that you really use.

Local brands and international brands

Buyers often ask whether to choose a local brand or an international one. There is no simple answer, and there is no need to treat it as a contest.

International brands often bring newer technology, wider model ranges and strong reputations. Local brands may offer lower prices, a nearby service network and parts that are easy to get. The best way to compare is by the exact model: the features, the warranty, the service availability and the long-term cost.

Ask the seller who services the product and how long parts are kept. Read owner feedback for the specific model. A good local product can beat a poor imported one, and the reverse can be true as well.

Buying well

  • Write down your needs and your space measurements before you shop.
  • Compare three models from at least two brands.
  • Check the full model code. Similar names can hide different versions.
  • Ask about the warranty, who provides it and how to claim.
  • Ask for delivery and installation terms in writing.
  • Check the product for damage at delivery, and keep the packaging for a while.
  • Register the warranty and keep the receipt in a safe place.
  • Read the manual before the first use.

Sales can be a good time to buy, but check that the discount is real. Compare the price with other shops, and watch for older models sold as new.

Looking after what you buy

Maintenance is cheap compared with repairs. Clean filters, door seals, drains and vents on a schedule. Descale in hard-water areas. Do not overload machines. Keep the manual and note the model and serial numbers.

Listen for changes in sound, watch for leaks and smell for burning. Small problems are cheaper to fix when you catch them early. If something seems wrong, stop using the appliance and call a service technician, especially if electricity or gas is involved.

When an appliance is old, think about whether repair makes sense. A rule of thumb is that if the repair costs more than half the price of a new equivalent, replacement may be wiser, particularly if the old one is inefficient.

Smart features: useful or not?

More appliances now connect to the internet and a phone app. A fridge can send an alert when the door is open. A washing machine can tell you the cycle is done. An air conditioner can be switched on before you get home.

Some of these are genuinely handy. Remote control of an air conditioner is useful in hot weather. Notifications from a robot vacuum save time. But smart features also bring costs. They need a stable network, they depend on the maker's app and servers, and they may stop working if the company ends support. They can also raise privacy questions, since the app collects data.

A sensible approach is to buy the appliance for how well it does its main job, and treat smart features as a bonus. If you do use them, keep the app updated, use a strong password and read what data the app collects.

Noise: what the numbers mean

Noise is measured in decibels, and the scale is not intuitive. An increase of about ten decibels sounds roughly twice as loud. So a fridge at forty decibels is much quieter than one at fifty.

For a bedroom air conditioner, an indoor unit in the low thirties of decibels at its lowest setting is very quiet. For a living-room fridge, the low forties are fine for most people. Washing machines are louder during the spin, and some models list separate figures for washing and spinning. Dishwashers and vacuum cleaners vary a lot.

Remember that the figure is measured under test conditions in a quiet room, and your home may sound different. Placement on a hard floor and against a wall can make noise worse. Where noise matters to you, read reviews that discuss it, and ask to hear the product in a shop if you can.

Saving electricity and water

Running costs can be cut without giving up comfort. A few habits make a real difference.

  • Set the air conditioner to a moderate temperature rather than the lowest. Every degree lower costs more.
  • Keep doors and windows closed when the air conditioner is running, and use curtains against strong sun.
  • Clean filters and coils so that machines do not have to work harder.
  • Keep the fridge reasonably full, but not packed, and do not leave the door open.
  • Let hot food cool before it goes in the fridge.
  • Run washing machines and dishwashers with full loads, and use cooler programmes for lightly soiled items.
  • Unplug chargers and devices that you do not use, and switch off standby where possible.

Small steps add up over a year, and the saving is greater for older machines that are less efficient.

Delivery day checklist

The day your appliance arrives is the best time to prevent problems.

  • Check the box for damage before the delivery team leaves. Note any damage in writing.
  • Open the product with the seller present if it is easy, and look for dents, scratches and missing parts.
  • Confirm that the model code matches your order.
  • Keep the manual, the warranty card and the accessories together.
  • For washing machines, remove the transport bolts before use. Forgetting them can damage the machine.
  • For fridges, many makers advise letting them stand upright for a few hours before switching on. Check the manual.
  • Test the product while the installer is still there, so that any early problem can be solved at once.

Take photos of the product and the serial number, and keep them with your receipt.

Renting, moving and small homes

If you rent a flat, you may not want to buy expensive appliances that you cannot take with you. Look for a landlord who provides the main ones, or buy reliable mid-range products that are easy to move. Check how the product ships, and keep the original packaging if possible.

When you move, prepare machines properly. Drain washing machines, secure the drums with transport bolts if you still have them, and keep fridges upright during transport. Have an air conditioner removed and refitted by professionals, because moving it incorrectly can lose refrigerant and void the warranty.

For small homes, compact appliances with stacked or built-in designs can save space. Consider a narrower washing machine, a slim dishwasher or a combined washer-dryer. These cost more per feature, but they make better use of the room.

What to do with the old one

Old appliances contain materials that should not go into ordinary rubbish. Fridges and air conditioners contain refrigerants and oils that need careful handling. Washing machines and other appliances contain metals and plastics that can be reused.

Ask the seller whether they take back your old appliance when they deliver the new one. Many do. If not, ask local services about proper disposal or recycling. If the old appliance still works, consider giving it to someone who needs it, or selling it. Before you do, clean it and make sure that it is safe to use.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Not measuring. A product that does not fit is a costly error.
  • Ignoring service. A great product with no repair network can become a problem.
  • Buying on features you will not use. Simple, reliable models are often best.
  • Skipping professional installation. It can affect performance and the warranty.
  • Forgetting running costs. Electricity and water add up over years.
  • Not checking the voltage. Especially for imported products.

Common questions

How long should an appliance last?

Many fridges and washing machines last ten years or more with good care. Air conditioners and vacuum cleaners often last a bit less. Build quality, usage and maintenance all matter.

Is inverter technology worth it?

For products that run for long hours, like air conditioners and fridges, usually yes. It saves electricity and often runs quietly.

Should I buy an extended warranty?

It can make sense for expensive products, but read what is covered and what is not. Often the basic warranty already covers the main risks.

Do I need a voltage stabiliser?

If your supply is unstable, a stabiliser or surge protector can protect expensive appliances. Ask a qualified electrician.
