MacBook or Windows laptop: a plain guide for 2026

Apple just launched a $599 MacBook. That single change moves this whole decision. Here is what actually matters now, in plain words, with a tool that matches a laptop to your budget and needs.

01

What changed this year

For a long time, "MacBook or Windows" was really a question of money first. A MacBook started around $1,000. A decent Windows laptop could be had for half that. In March 2026, Apple broke that pattern.

Apple launched the MacBook Neo, its first budget MacBook, at $599. It uses the same aluminum body and keyboard as the pricier MacBooks. Inside is the same chip found in the iPhone 16 Pro. Apple built it to compete directly with cheap Windows laptops and Chromebooks, especially for students.

At the same time, Windows laptops have also moved. Microsoft has a program called Copilot+ PC. These laptops use chips that run AI features on the device itself. Entry models now start around $600 to $800. So both sides now offer a real, usable laptop under $800. The choice is no longer only about price. It is about what each side does well.

Words explained
  • Copilot+ PC: a Windows laptop with a chip strong enough to run some AI features directly on the device, without needing the internet for every task.
  • Apple Silicon: Apple's own chips, called M-series (M4, M5, and so on) in most MacBooks, built by Apple instead of Intel or AMD.
  • RAM: the laptop's short-term memory. More RAM lets you keep more apps open at once without slowing down.
  • SSD: the storage where your files and apps live. Bigger means more space for photos, videos and programs.

On the Windows side, there is no single "starting price" the way there is for a MacBook. Dozens of brands sell laptops at every price point. The useful dividing line is the Copilot+ label. A laptop with that label has a chip strong enough for on-device AI features and usually offers strong battery life. Below about $600, most Windows laptops do not carry that label, and they can feel slow within a year or two.

Part 2What actually differsSpeed, software, repairs, gaming and battery
04

Speed and chips

In short: Apple's chips are known for strong speed without draining the battery fast. Windows laptops now offer similar efficient chips too, but only in the Copilot+ models, so check for that label.

Read the details on chips and speed

Apple designs its own chips, called M-series, and puts them in every current MacBook. The newest is the M5. Reviewers have measured its graphics as up to a third faster than the previous M4 chip. Apple also built in stronger support for on-device AI tasks.

The cheapest MacBook, the Neo, uses a different chip. It has an A18 Pro, the same chip family used in the iPhone 16 Pro, not a full M-series chip. Apple says it still beats a typical Intel Core Ultra 5 Windows laptop at everyday tasks. Even so, it is the weakest chip in Apple's current lineup. Its memory is fixed at 8GB, with no way to add more later.

On the Windows side, speed depends heavily on which chip is inside, because many different companies make them. The newest chips built for battery efficiency come from three makers. Qualcomm makes the Snapdragon X series. Intel makes Core Ultra chips, including Lunar Lake and the newer Panther Lake. AMD makes the Ryzen AI 300 series. These are the chips found in Copilot+ PCs. An older or cheaper Windows laptop may use a weaker chip that runs hotter and drains the battery faster.

The practical rule: on a MacBook, any current model will feel smooth for normal use. On a Windows laptop, check the chip name before you buy. Look for the Copilot+ PC label, or a recent Snapdragon X, Intel Core Ultra, or Ryzen AI chip. A laptop with one of these will behave much more like a MacBook in daily use than an older or budget chip will.

05

Software, apps and your other devices

In short: most normal software runs on both. The real difference is your other devices. An iPhone works more smoothly with a MacBook. Most work software is built with Windows in mind first.

Read the details on software and ecosystem

For browsing, email, video calls, streaming and most office work, both sides run the same major apps well. Google's apps, Microsoft's own Office apps, Zoom, Spotify and most other everyday software work on either system.

Where it splits is specialist software. Some professional tools are built for Windows first. So are almost all PC games. Both may run poorly on a Mac, or not at all. On the other side, a small number of Apple-only tools, like Final Cut Pro for video editing, exist only on macOS.

The bigger factor for most people is what else they own. If you have an iPhone, a MacBook unlocks a few conveniences. You can copy text on one device and paste it on the other. You can answer phone calls from the laptop. You can send files nearby instantly with AirDrop. None of these are essential, but people who already live in Apple's world tend to enjoy them. A Windows laptop works fine with an iPhone too, just without those shortcuts.

Your workplace or school may already issue Windows laptops, or use software that only runs on Windows. If so, that alone can decide the question for you, whatever else this page says.

06

Ports, upgrades and repairs

In short: most MacBooks cannot be upgraded later. Many Windows laptops let you add storage, and some let you add memory too. Check this before you buy if you want a laptop to last.

Read the details on ports and repairs

On a current MacBook, the memory and storage are built into the main circuit board. You choose the amount when you buy it, and you cannot add more later. This makes buying a little more important to get right, since your choice at checkout is permanent. The MacBook Neo is the strictest example: its 8GB of memory cannot be changed at all, on any configuration.

Many Windows laptops are more flexible. A good number still let you open the bottom panel and add a bigger storage drive later. Some, mostly larger and thicker models, let you add more memory too. This varies a lot by brand and model. If upgrading later matters to you, check the exact laptop's specification page, not just the brand name.

Ports follow a similar pattern. Apple's MacBook Air and Pro models include a good spread of USB-C ports. The Pro adds a full-size HDMI port and a memory card slot, useful for photographers. The MacBook Neo is more basic, with two USB-C ports, and one of them runs at a slower speed. Windows laptops vary widely here too. The thinnest models offer very few ports. Larger ones offer a full set, including USB-A, HDMI and card readers.

Both sides have official repair programs. Both can be expensive outside of warranty. A screen or a logic board replacement is costly work on any thin laptop. So many brands make Windows laptops that a local repair shop is more likely to know your model well. This still depends on where you live.

07

Gaming

In short: Windows is still clearly ahead for gaming, because almost every big game is made for it first. A MacBook can play some games well, but the library is much smaller.

Read the details on gaming

If gaming matters to you at all, this is usually the deciding factor. Windows has been the main platform for PC gaming for decades, and nearly every major new game is built for it first. Stores like Steam offer a huge library that runs on Windows without any extra work.

Apple has been investing in Mac gaming, and its newer chips handle modern games better than older Macs could. Apple even provides a tool that helps some Windows games run on a Mac. Even so, far fewer games run well on a Mac than on Windows. A MacBook also has no dedicated gaming graphics chip, unlike a gaming laptop.

If you want to play new, demanding games, a Windows laptop, or better yet, a dedicated gaming laptop, is the safer choice. If you play only casual games, mobile-style games, or a handful of older titles, a MacBook can usually manage. For a deeper look at gaming-focused laptops, see our separate guide to gaming laptops.

08

Battery life

In short: MacBooks have led on battery life for years. Copilot+ Windows laptops have closed much of that gap, and some have matched or beaten MacBooks in independent tests.

Read the details on battery life

Apple claims up to 18 hours for the MacBook Air, and even more for some MacBook Pro models. MacBooks have had a strong real-world reputation for battery life for years. The MacBook Neo costs less, but it is still rated for up to 16 hours. It shares the same efficient approach to power.

For a long time, Windows laptops could not keep up. That has changed with Copilot+ PCs. Laptop Mag tested one early Snapdragon-powered Windows laptop and found it lasted over 19 hours. That beat some MacBook Pro models at the time. Not every Copilot+ laptop reaches that level. Most still land comfortably over 10 hours, enough for a full day away from a charger for most people.

The laptops that still struggle on battery are older or budget Windows models without the Copilot+ label. If battery life matters and you are considering a Windows laptop, treat the Copilot+ label as a helpful filter, not a promise. Check independent battery test results for the exact model before you buy.

Part 3Making the choiceWho should buy which, and how to buy here
09

Who should buy which

Lean toward a MacBook if

  • You already use an iPhone or iPad daily
  • You want strong battery life without checking every spec sheet
  • You do photo or video editing
  • You want fewer decisions and a simple, consistent design

Lean toward Windows if

  • You play PC games, especially new releases
  • Your school or workplace requires Windows software
  • You want to add more storage or memory later
  • You want the widest possible choice of price and design

Four short cases

The student on a tight budget. The MacBook Neo or an entry Copilot+ Windows laptop both work well for browsing, documents and video calls. Pick whichever matches the phone and accounts you already use.

The office and home user. A MacBook Air or a mainstream Copilot+ Windows laptop both handle normal work comfortably. Check what software your job actually requires before you decide.

The photo or video editor. A MacBook Pro is a strong, well-tested choice for this. A premium Windows laptop with a strong graphics chip is a fair alternative. This is especially true if your editing software favors Windows.

The gamer. Choose Windows, and look at our separate guide to gaming laptops for details on graphics chips and prices.

10

Buying in Uzbekistan

Every price above is a US price, so your local shop will look different. Working through the checklist below can spare you an expensive surprise.

  • Confirm the warranty in writing. Buying from an official Apple or Windows-brand seller usually means local repair support. A grey-market laptop can look like a bargain at checkout and turn expensive the moment it needs fixing.
  • Check the exact model and chip. Two laptops with the same name can carry different chips or memory in different countries. Match the model number on the box to the maker's own specification page.
  • Confirm the keyboard layout. Some regional MacBook and Windows laptop keyboards differ in small ways. Check this if you plan to type a lot.
  • Ask about the charger. Confirm the plug and voltage match our mains supply, or plan to buy a proper adapter.
  • Decide on memory and storage before you buy a MacBook. Remember that most MacBooks cannot be upgraded later, so choose a little more than you think you need.
FAQ

Questions people ask

Is the $599 MacBook Neo actually a good deal?

For light use, yes. It has a real aluminum MacBook design and a capable chip. Its memory is fixed at 8GB and cannot be upgraded. It suits browsing, study and office work better than heavy multitasking or editing.

What does Copilot+ PC actually mean?

It is Microsoft's label for a Windows laptop with a strong enough chip. That chip can run certain AI features on the device itself, without needing the internet. These laptops also tend to have longer battery life than older Windows models.

Can a MacBook run Windows programs?

Not directly, and not officially anymore on Apple Silicon MacBooks. Some Windows-only software has no direct alternative on a Mac. Check whether the specific programs you need have a Mac version before you switch.

Which lasts longer, a MacBook or a Windows laptop?

MacBooks still have a strong reputation for lasting several years, mainly thanks to long software support. A well-chosen Copilot+ Windows laptop can also last well. But the range of quality across Windows laptops is much wider, so the specific model matters more.

Should I buy a MacBook Air or a MacBook Pro?

Choose the Air if you mainly browse, study or do office work. Choose the Pro if you edit video or photos, or do heavy coding. It also suits you if you want a brighter screen, smoother motion, and better cooling for long tasks.
