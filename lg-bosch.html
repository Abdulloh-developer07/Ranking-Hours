LG or Bosch: which washing machine will you be happier with?

Two of the most common brands in a laundry room, with different habits. One loves features and quick cycles, the other has a reputation for steady, quiet work. Here is what is true and what is just talk.

By Abdulloh Mahmudov  |  Updated October 2026  |  About 15 minute read

LG

Often a good fit if

You like a lot of programs and smart options, want steam or app control, or want a wide range of capacities, including top-loading models.

Bosch

Often a good fit if

You want a calm, simple machine with a reputation for quiet running, solid build and sensible cycles, and you will keep it for many years.

How to read this page

When it is time to buy a new washing machine, most of us decide from what the salesperson says or from a brochure full of numbers. In daily life you need only a few things: the machine should clean clothes properly, treat delicate fabrics gently, last for years without trouble and not shake the kitchen floor during a night cycle. LG from South Korea and Bosch from Germany are two brands that people argue about for exactly those reasons.

We have not tested individual machines in a lab, and both brands sell dozens of models that change every year or two. So this page does not crown a winner. It explains the common differences, the technical words you will meet in a shop, and the checks you can do on the exact model you are considering. Details like capacity, spin speed, energy use and warranty are printed on each model's label and on the maker's page, and those figures, not our summary, are what you should rely on.

Drum size and real loads

Washing machines are sold by capacity in kilograms of dry laundry, usually somewhere between 6 and 12 kg. That number is the maximum for a simple cotton load. In daily life you will rarely reach it, and you should not try to. A drum stuffed to the top washes badly, because the clothes cannot move and fall through the water, and it is harder on the bearings and the suspension.

CapacitySuitsReal-life note
6 to 7 kgOne or two peopleFine for daily small loads, tight for bedding
8 to 9 kgA family of three or fourThe most popular range, handles a duvet cover and a few towels
10 to 12 kgA large family or heavy laundryNeeds more space and water, and may not run well with tiny loads

A good rule is to fill the drum to about three quarters, leaving room for the clothes to tumble. Both brands sell machines across most of these sizes. LG tends to offer more very large models, while Bosch offers a strong choice in the middle sizes. Compare the drum diameter and the door opening too. A wider door makes loading and unloading much easier, especially if you have a bad back or small children.

Motors and drive systems

Almost all current mid-range and premium machines from both brands use an inverter motor, a brushless design with no carbon brushes that wear out. It runs more quietly, uses less energy and can change speed smoothly. The marketing names differ: LG calls its version Inverter Direct Drive, and Bosch uses the name EcoSilence Drive for its brushless motor. The core idea is similar.

What differs is how the motor is connected to the drum. In a direct-drive layout the motor sits at the back and turns the drum directly. There is no belt to wear out or slip. In a belt-drive layout, a rubber belt connects a motor to a pulley on the drum. Belts are cheap and easy to replace, and a belt drive is not a bad design, but it is one more part that can wear. Different models from both brands use different arrangements, so do not assume. Ask the seller, or check the manual, whether the model in front of you uses a belt, and what the warranty says about the motor.

Good to know: a brushless motor is a long-life part, but the electronic board that controls it is the part that most often needs repair in any modern machine. Ask how long the board and motor are covered, not only the "whole machine."

Noise and vibration

Noise is quoted in decibels, usually separately for washing and for spinning. A machine might be around 50 dB when washing and 70 dB or more when spinning. Every three decibels is a clear step, and ten decibels sounds roughly twice as loud. The spin is the noisy part, and the faster the drum spins, the more noise and vibration it makes.

Both brands advertise quiet operation, and in practice both can be very quiet when installed properly. Bosch is often praised for its steady, low-noise running and its vibration-damping design, with side panels that reduce shaking. LG's direct-drive machines are generally quiet as well, and the brand stresses low vibration. The real difference between two good machines at home is usually smaller than the difference you cause by installing it badly.

What causes a noisy machine at home

  • Transport bolts left in. These metal bolts at the back lock the drum during delivery. If they stay in, the machine will bang and may be damaged. Always remove them and keep them for future moves.
  • Uneven feet. Use a spirit level and adjust the feet until the machine does not rock when you push the corners.
  • A weak floor. Wooden or old floors can amplify vibration. A thick rubber mat or a concrete plate under the machine helps.
  • An unbalanced load. One heavy towel with a few light items makes the drum wobble. Mix sizes.
  • Coins and hairpins in the drum. Check the pockets.

Programs and features

This is where brand names get confusing, because the same idea has different names. Here are the common features and what they do in simple terms.

FeatureWhat it really doesWorth it?
Steam cyclesAdds steam to relax creases or refresh clothes, and in some programs to help with hygieneUseful if you wear shirts or dislike ironing
Quick wash (15 to 30 minutes)A short cycle for lightly worn clothesVery useful for small loads
Automatic detergent dosingThe machine holds a tank of detergent and doses it for each load, found on higher-end models of both brandsHandy and saves detergent, but you must refill the tank and use the right kind
Load sensingThe machine weighs the load and adjusts water and timeGood, and common in both
Allergy or hygiene programsHot wash with extra rinseHelpful for bedding and baby clothes
Drum cleaning programA hot cycle that cleans the drum and sealRun it once a month
AI or sensor programsThe machine picks a cycle based on the fabric it detectsNice if it works well, but a manual choice does the same job

LG usually offers more programs and more options on a single machine, such as extra steam and special cycles. Bosch tends to offer fewer, simpler options that are well tuned, along with features like dosing and a quick program on higher models. If you like lots of buttons, LG may feel more fun. If you want to turn the dial and forget it, Bosch can feel more relaxed. Neither is better; they are different personalities.

In daily use most households use three or four programs: cotton at 40 degrees, a mixed cycle, a quick wash and a delicate or wool cycle. Count how many programs you will really use before you pay for a long list.

Water and energy

Modern washing machines use far less water and electricity than older ones, and the label shows how much. The current European-style label gives an energy class on a scale from A to G, along with litres per cycle and kilowatt-hours per 100 cycles. The scale was redrawn in recent years, so a machine rated C or D today may be as efficient as an older machine that carried an A.

Compare these figures between models with the same capacity. A bigger drum can use more water in a cycle, but a machine that washes more clothes per cycle may use less per kilo. The real savings come from habits: wash full loads, use 30 or 40 degrees for most clothes, and skip the extra heating cycles when not needed. Heating the water is the biggest part of the electricity use, so lower temperatures save the most.

If your water supply has low pressure, check the minimum water pressure in the specifications. Some machines fill slowly or give errors if the pressure is low, and a small booster pump or a tank can fix it.

Hard water and limescale

In many regions the tap water is hard, which means it has a lot of dissolved calcium and magnesium. When heated, it leaves scale on the heating element and the drum. Scale shortens the life of a machine more than almost anything else. It is a bigger issue than which brand you pick.

  • Use detergent that is made for your water hardness, and follow the dose on the pack. Overdosing leaves residue.
  • Use a descaling product for washing machines every few months if your water is hard.
  • Run the drum clean cycle at a high temperature regularly.
  • Consider a water filter or softener at the inlet, if the water in your building is very hard.
  • Clean the small mesh filters on the water hoses once or twice a year. They catch sand and rust that can block the valves.

If you are not sure about your water, a cheap test strip from a pet or garden shop will give you a rough idea.

Apps and connectivity

Both brands offer Wi-Fi models that connect to a phone app. LG has an app called ThinQ, and Bosch uses Home Connect. They let you start a cycle remotely, get a notification when the wash is done, download new programs and check for faults. This is nice, but it is not essential, and it depends on a stable home network and on the maker continuing to support the app. A machine that is excellent without the app is better than one that is only good with it.

If you do want it, check that the app works in your country and language before you buy, and read recent reviews. Features sometimes differ between markets.

Front load, top load and washer-dryer combos

Front load

The most common type in Europe and a lot of Asia. It uses less water and washes gently with the tumbling action, and the top of the machine can be used as a shelf or fitted under a counter. Both brands sell many front-loading models.

Top load

Easier to load without bending, and a good fit for narrow spaces, because the door is on top. LG sells top-loading machines in some markets, while Bosch focuses mainly on front loaders. Top loaders can be a little rougher on clothes and use more water, though modern ones are better.

Washer-dryer combos

One drum washes and dries. They save space, but the dryer capacity is usually smaller than the wash capacity, and the drying takes a long time. For a small flat they can be a lifesaver. For a family, separate machines are more practical. Check the real drying capacity, which is often about half of the wash capacity.

Service, parts and warranty

A washing machine is a mechanical product with a motor, pump, bearings, valves and an electronic board, and something will eventually wear. Before choosing a brand, find out what happens when that day comes.

  • How long is the warranty on the whole machine, and how long on the motor and the drum bearings?
  • Where is the nearest authorised service centre, and how long does a visit usually take?
  • Are the common spare parts, such as pumps, door locks, seals and bearings, available locally, and for how long after the model is discontinued?
  • Does the warranty need installation by a certified technician?
  • What does a repair roughly cost compared to the machine?

Both brands have service networks in many places, but the quality and speed differ between cities and importers. Ask neighbours and friends who own either brand how their last repair went, because that is more reliable than any brochure. Keep your receipt, the warranty card and a photo of the model sticker.

Price and value

In general, Bosch machines cost a little more than LG machines of similar capacity, and LG tends to offer more features per amount of money. That is a pattern, not a rule, and it changes with sales and each shop's pricing. Exact prices change often and differ by country, so we do not quote any.

  • Compare machines of the same capacity and spin speed.
  • Remember delivery, installation and hoses, which may cost extra.
  • If you plan to keep the machine for ten years, a slightly higher price for a longer motor warranty can make sense.
  • Be careful with the cheapest model in a series, which often lacks the features advertised for the range.
  • Check any instalment plan for interest and fees.

Which one for whom

A busy family with lots of laundry

An 8 to 10 kg front loader with load sensing and a quick program. Either brand works, and the decision comes down to service and price.

A flat with a thin wall or a baby sleeping next door

Prioritise low spin noise and a solid installation. Look at the decibel figures for spinning and choose a lower spin speed when you wash at night.

Someone who loves features

LG's range of programs, steam and app options will probably give you more to play with.

Someone who wants simplicity and a long life

Bosch's straightforward programs and calm character suit this approach, but check the motor and belt details on the model.

A small space

Measure carefully. A slim front loader or a top loader may fit where a standard unit does not. Check the depth, because some large drums need 60 cm or more.

Hard water area

Take the descaling routine seriously with any brand, and consider a water filter.

In the shop

  • Read the label for capacity, spin speed, noise, water use and energy class.
  • Open the door and look at the opening size, the seal and the drum surface.
  • Check the detergent drawer, which should pull out smoothly and be easy to clean.
  • Look at the control panel. A dial and clear buttons are easier to use than a complicated touch screen.
  • Ask about the motor warranty and the nearest service centre.
  • Ask whether the machine on display is the same as the one you will receive, and when it was made.
  • When it is delivered, check for dents and open the box with the courier present.

Setup and care

  • Remove all transport bolts and level the machine before the first wash.
  • Use the hoses that came with the machine, and check that the connections are tight with no leaks.
  • Run an empty hot cycle once before the first wash to clear any factory residue.
  • After each wash, leave the door and the detergent drawer slightly open, so the inside dries and does not smell.
  • Wipe the door seal now and then and remove hairs and lint.
  • Clean the drain pump filter every one to three months, with a towel under it, because some water will come out.
  • Run a hot drum clean cycle once a month, and use less detergent than the pack suggests if the clothes feel stiff.
  • Do not overload the drum or wash heavy items like thick rugs in a machine that is not meant for them.

Questions people ask

Is LG or Bosch more reliable?

Both can last for many years, and both have models that give owners trouble. Reliability depends on the model, the installation, the water and the way you use it. Look at the warranty on the motor and the quality of local service rather than trusting a brand reputation alone.

Which brand is quieter?

Both offer quiet machines. Bosch has a long reputation for quiet running, and LG's direct-drive machines are also quiet. A well-leveled machine on a solid floor makes more difference than the logo.

What spin speed do I need?

Between 1000 and 1400 revolutions per minute is common. Higher speeds leave clothes drier, which saves drying time, but they are louder and slightly harder on delicate fabrics. For most people 1200 is a fair choice.

Do I need a Wi-Fi washing machine?

No. It is a convenience, not a necessity. Choose it only if you will use it.

Why does my machine smell?

Usually from damp residue in the drum seal and the detergent drawer, plus too much detergent and washing only at low temperatures. Leave the door open after use, clean the seal and filter, and run a hot clean cycle.

Can I fix it myself?

Simple jobs like cleaning the pump filter and the inlet mesh are fine. For electronics, the motor and the bearings, call a technician, and do not do it during the warranty period.

Is a more expensive washing machine always better?

Not always. Extra money often buys features, a nicer finish and bigger capacity. If you do not use the extras, a mid-range model with a good warranty is usually the best value.

How long should a washing machine last?

Ten years is a reasonable goal for a well-looked-after machine, though it can be shorter with hard water, heavy use or poor installation.

The short checklist

  • Choose a capacity that matches your real loads.
  • Check the motor type and the motor warranty.
  • Look at the spin noise and install the machine properly.
  • Count the programs you will really use.
  • Plan for hard water.
  • Ask about service and spare parts before you pay.
