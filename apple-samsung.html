1. Design and Build

Apple has stuck with its signature square screen since the very first Apple Watch. The reasoning makes sense - a square shape fits text and data more naturally than a circle, without cutting off the edges of what you're reading. The case comes in recycled aluminum or surgical-grade stainless steel, both built to handle daily bumps and scratches.

Samsung went the traditional route with a classic round watch face on the Galaxy Watch 6, made from Armor Aluminum for extra durability during workouts. The round design gives it a more classic look that works just as well running trails as it does in a business meeting. Samsung also trimmed the bezels down this generation, giving the screen a bit more usable space.

2. Display Quality

Apple's Series 9 uses an Always-On Retina OLED display that hits 2000 nits of peak brightness - genuinely readable in direct sunlight, which is where a lot of smartwatch screens struggle. It can also dim down to just 1 nit in the dark, so it won't blind you if you check it at night.

Samsung matches that brightness with its own Super AMOLED circular display, also hitting 2000 nits, with sharp enough resolution that text and workout charts look crisp and clean. It's protected by sapphire crystal, so it resists scratches well. Touch response feels instant on both watches - scrolling, tapping, and navigating menus feels equally smooth on either one.

3. Chips and Performance

The Apple Watch Series 9 runs on Apple's new S9 chip, which packs in a 4-core Neural Engine. That means Siri requests get processed right on the watch instead of being sent to the cloud - which makes voice commands faster and keeps your data more private.

The Galaxy Watch 6 runs Samsung's Exynos W930 dual-core chip at 1.4GHz, paired with 2GB of RAM and 16GB of storage. It's tuned more for battery efficiency than raw speed, but in daily use it switches between apps and watch faces smoothly, even while streaming music offline during a run.

4. Battery Life

The Apple Watch Series 9 gets around 18 hours per charge - enough for a full day, but you'll need to charge it nightly, which can be annoying if you also want to track your sleep. Apple's fast charging helps here; a quick charge in the morning or evening keeps it topped up.

The Galaxy Watch 6 lasts significantly longer - up to 40 hours with the Always-On Display turned off, or around 30 hours with it on. That's enough for multi-day use, meaning you can track a full night's sleep and still make it through a workday without worrying about the battery. This is one of Samsung's biggest advantages if you don't want to charge your watch every night.

5. Health Tracking Features

The Apple Watch Series 9 packs in a serious set of health sensors. Its ECG app lets you take a single-lead reading on demand, which helps flag things like atrial fibrillation early. It also tracks your wrist temperature overnight, which feeds into cycle tracking and helps estimate ovulation windows over time.

Samsung takes a similar approach with its 3-in-1 BioActive sensor, which combines heart rate, electrical heart signal, and body composition sensors into one chip. The standout feature here is the body composition scan - in about 15 seconds, it estimates your muscle mass, body fat percentage, and water levels, similar to what you'd normally need a dedicated scale for.

6. Sleep Tracking

The Apple Watch breaks your night down into REM, Core, and Deep sleep stages inside the Health app, along with tracking wake-ups, breathing rate, and wrist temperature shifts - giving you a clear picture of how well you actually recovered overnight.

Samsung adds a more interactive layer with its Sleep Coaching feature. After a few nights of tracking, it assigns you a "Sleep Animal" type based on your patterns, then gives you a month-long coaching plan with daily tips to improve your sleep. It also connects to SmartThings, so it can dim your bedroom lights automatically once it detects you've fallen asleep.

7. Fitness and Workout Tracking

The Apple Watch Series 9 automatically recognizes common workouts, from running to HIIT, and shows real-time heart rate zones so you know whether you're in an easy aerobic pace or pushing hard. It also supports track running with precise lap and distance tracking for more serious runners.

The Galaxy Watch 6 supports over 95 workout types, including swimming and indoor rowing, and calculates five personalized heart rate zones based on your fitness level. It gives you real-time feedback through vibrations so you can stay in your target zone, and it's quick to auto-detect a workout even if you forget to hit start.

8. Software: watchOS vs Wear OS

watchOS 10 introduced a Smart Stack of widgets you can access by turning the Digital Crown - handy for quickly checking things like your calendar or the weather. Navigation feels smooth throughout, and Apple's App Store has a wide range of well-optimized third-party apps.

The Galaxy Watch 6 runs Wear OS 4 with Samsung's One UI layered on top, which gives you a lot more freedom to customize your layout, widgets, and watch face. Since it's built on Google's platform, you also get native access to Google Maps, Google Wallet, and YouTube Music right from your wrist.

9. Working With Your Other Devices

The Apple Watch fits neatly into the Apple ecosystem - it can unlock your Mac automatically, help you find a misplaced iPhone with precise directional tracking, control your Apple TV, and let you check security cameras or smart lights through the Home app.

The Galaxy Watch 6 does the same for Android and SmartThings devices - syncing with Galaxy phones, tablets, and TVs. You can use it as a remote camera shutter, show workout stats on your TV during a streamed class, or trigger smart home routines like locking doors based on your location.

10. Which One Should You Buy?

Really, this comes down to which phone you already have. The Apple Watch Series 9 starts at $399 and is the clear choice if you're on iPhone - polished software, accurate health tracking, and deep iOS integration. The Galaxy Watch 6 starts at $299 and offers excellent value for Android users, with much longer battery life and genuinely useful body composition tracking. Either one is a great smartwatch - just make sure it matches your phone.

Quick Verdict

Best for iPhone Users

The clear pick if you're an iPhone owner - smooth software, a huge app selection, and the best notification handling with your iPhone.

When to choose: If you are an iPhone owner looking for fluid system response, top-tier build value, and pristine ecosystem harmony.

Best for Android Users

Built for Android users who want more out of their watch - roughly double the battery life, deep Android integration, and multi-day tracking without nightly charging.

When to choose: If you want massive battery capacity, value for money, and use any standard Android or Samsung ecosystem smartphone.

Which One Fits Your Life?

If you're on iPhone, the Apple Watch is the obvious pick - nothing else integrates as well. If you're on Android, the Galaxy Watch 6 is the better fit. And if battery life matters most to you specifically, Samsung wins by a wide margin.
