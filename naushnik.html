2. Noise Cancellation

We tested both across a range of noise, from low engine rumble to office chatter. Sony's multiple microphones pick up a wide range of sound and are especially good at killing low-frequency noise - great for flights and subway commutes. It also adjusts based on how the headphones fit your ears, avoiding that uncomfortable pressure feeling some ANC headphones cause.

Apple uses its H2 chip to specifically target trickier mid-range sounds, like voices and coffee shop chatter. Where Apple really stands out is Transparency Mode - it pipes outside sound back into your ears with almost no delay, making it feel like you're not wearing headphones at all. That's genuinely useful if you're running outside or need to stay aware of your surroundings at work.

3. Sound Quality

For serious listeners, wireless bandwidth affects how good your music actually sounds. Sony has a real edge here on Android with its LDAC codec, which streams high-resolution audio at up to 990kbps - keeping lossless tracks from Tidal or Apple Music detailed and well-separated. It also has DSEE Extreme, which uses AI to add back detail to compressed MP3s in real time.

Apple sticks with AAC, which can't match LDAC's bitrate, but its custom drivers keep distortion extremely low even at high volumes. The sound is well-balanced right out of the box - deep bass that doesn't overpower vocals. For movies, Apple's Dolby Atmos head-tracking genuinely adds a sense of 3D space that regular stereo can't match.

4. Build Quality and Comfort

Sony uses recycled plastics and lightweight materials, keeping the WH-1000XM5 at just 250 grams. The headband slides smoothly, and the soft earcups are comfortable even with glasses on during long sessions. The one downside: it doesn't fold flat, so you'll need the included case to pack it for travel.

Apple goes for a more premium feel - machined aluminum, steel arms, and a breathable mesh headband - but that comes at a cost: 385 grams, noticeably heavier than the Sony. That extra weight can cause some neck fatigue during long editing sessions. The earcups attach magnetically, which makes them easy to clean or swap out, though the exposed headband is more prone to scratches.

Which One Should You Get?

Go With Sony If:

  • You want the best lossless streaming quality on Android or PC.
  • You want the lightest possible headphones for long sessions.
  • Your main priority is blocking out low-frequency engine noise.
  • You want deep, customizable EQ controls.

Go With Apple If:

  • You're deeply invested in the Apple ecosystem.
  • You prefer premium materials like aluminum over plastic.
  • You watch a lot of movies and want spatial audio with head tracking.
  • You want the most natural-sounding transparency mode available.
