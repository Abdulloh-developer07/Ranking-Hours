Dell XPS 14 vs Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Carbon: the brand that came back

Dell dropped the XPS name in 2025 and brought it back in 2026. Prices have jumped around ever since. Here is how the revived XPS 14 compares with the ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 14.

01

Why this comparison needed a rewrite

If you searched for this comparison a year ago, you would not have found a Dell XPS to compare. Dell had dropped the name.

At CES 2025, Dell scrapped XPS, Inspiron and Latitude. A confusing new system replaced them. Consumers got Dell, Dell Plus and Dell Premium. Business buyers got Dell Pro and Dell Pro Max. Many people disliked the change. Ars Technica called it a poor decision.

A year later, Dell reversed course. At CES 2026 it brought XPS back as its premium consumer brand. Dell vice chairman Jeff Clarke told reporters, "I owe you an apology." The company launched a redesigned XPS 14 and XPS 16. It said an XPS 13 would follow later in the year.

Jan 2026XPS returns at CES. The XPS 14 goes on sale from $2,050, after Dell raised the price by about $400 just before launch.
Feb 2026Dell adds cheaper setups. The entry Core Ultra 5 model is listed at $1,349.
May 2026Dell raises prices again. It points to component costs.

The Lenovo side is far less dramatic. ThinkPad has kept its name, its black design and its identity for decades. One brand rebuilt itself twice in two years. The other barely changed. Keep that contrast in mind as you read on.

02

Prices in 2026: a moving target

In short: Both laptops cost more in mid-2026 than early in the year. A memory shortage is pushing prices up. Treat every price here as a snapshot, and check the current listing.

Read the price details

Laptop prices rose in 2026. AI data centres are buying huge amounts of memory and storage chips. Reports call the shortage "RAMageddon." A Dell spokesperson told Gizmodo it has been "a dynamic time in our industry for component costs."

ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 14: higher at retail

Lenovo said the X1 Carbon Aura Edition would start at $1,999 in the US. HotHardware listed $2,139 for the version it reviewed, and $2,334 as tested.

By August, retailer listings looked higher. One price tracker found a 32GB build at about $2,540 at Amazon. It found a touch model with Windows 11 Pro at about $3,190 at CDW. It listed OLED versions at about $2,750 at Newegg and $2,900 at B&H. In the US, retail builds were limited to 32GB of memory.

What this means

The entry XPS still costs less than a ThinkPad. But it has only 16GB of memory and a smaller chip. Match the two more closely and the gap almost disappears.

The top XPS OLED build with 32GB and 1TB was $2,879.99 in May. OLED ThinkPads with 32GB listed for about $2,750 to $2,900 in August. The chips differ, so this is not an exact match. Still, at that level the two cost about the same.

A money-saving tipLast year's XPS 14 is an older design. Dell sold it briefly as the "Dell 14 Premium" and then renamed it. In August 2026, Windows Central saw that model with 32GB, 1TB and a 3.2K OLED touch screen for $1,549.99. It had been $2,899.99. The chips are one generation older. The saving is large. Ask sellers about older stock, and check the exact specs.

04

Design and weight

In short: Both look sharp. The ThinkPad is much lighter. The XPS is closer to a MacBook Pro in style.

Read the details on design and weight

The new XPS 14 puts the XPS logo on the lid. It has a CNC-machined aluminum body that is just 14.6 mm thin. Tom's Guide says that sits between the MacBook Air M4 (11.3 mm) and the MacBook Pro M5 (15.5 mm). The same reviewer calls it a 14-inch laptop in the size of a 13-inch one.

It weighs about 3 pounds. That is half a pound less than the previous XPS 14. Reviewers have compared its clean, minimal look to a MacBook Pro.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 14 goes the other way. It weighs 2.15 pounds, about 980 grams. That is very light for a 14-inch laptop.

It still looks like a ThinkPad: black and understated. The red TrackPoint nub is still there. This year's model adds a "Space Frame" design. It uses an inner frame that makes the laptop easier to open and repair.

If you carry a laptop every day, a gap of nearly a pound matters. You will feel it. It is not just a number on a spec sheet.

05

Keyboard and trackpad

In short: ThinkPad keyboards have a long reputation for comfort. The new XPS fixes two features people disliked.

Read the details on the keyboard and trackpad

Earlier XPS models had a touch strip instead of real function keys. They also had a trackpad with no visible edge. Many reviewers disliked both. TechRadar says the 2026 model addresses these divisive features.

The ThinkPad keyboard has been a selling point for decades. It has deep key travel, and many long-time users rate it very highly. LaptopMedia's review calls the keyboard excellent.

The Gen 14 can also be ordered with a haptic trackpad. This simulates a click using vibration, instead of a moving switch. The TrackPoint, the little red joystick, is still there too. It suits people who do not want to lift their hands off the keys.

If typing feel matters most, the ThinkPad still has the stronger reputation. The XPS has closed part of the gap.

06

Ports and repairs

In short: The ThinkPad has more ports. The XPS has three USB-C ports and a headphone jack. The ThinkPad is also designed to be easier to open.

Read the details on ports and repairs

TechRadar lists the XPS 14 ports as three USB-C and a 3.5 mm headphone jack. There is no HDMI port and no card slot. The MacBook Pro 14 has both. If you connect to projectors, TVs or cameras, plan to buy an adapter or a dock.

The ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 14 offers up to three Thunderbolt 4 or USB4 ports, plus HDMI, according to Lenovo's specs. Its Space Frame design makes the laptop easier to open. One review site says this can reduce the scope of common repairs.

Memory is soldered on both laptops. You cannot add more later. Choose enough when you buy.

07

Speed, graphics and battery

In short: Both use Intel's new Panther Lake chips and count as Copilot+ PCs. The ThinkPad is not class-leading for speed, but it holds its power well. Battery claims differ a lot.

Read the details on speed and battery

Both laptops use Intel's Core Ultra Series 3 chips, code-named Panther Lake. Both carry the Copilot+ PC label. That means the chip can run some AI features on the device itself.

Speed

The Gadgeteer tested a ThinkPad with the Core Ultra 7 355. It scored 2,621 single-core and about 11,200 multi-core in Geekbench 6. The reviewer called that a real jump over last year's chip, but not class-leading. Lenovo says a 70% larger fan lets the chassis hold about 30 watts of sustained power.

Dell says it redesigned the XPS cooling. It says graphics performance is up to 50% better. The strongest Intel Arc graphics come with the top Core Ultra X chips. In Dell's store, that step cost about $450 in spring 2026.

Neither laptop is built for gaming. For that, see our guide to gaming laptops.

Battery

Dell claims up to 27 hours on the XPS 14 with the standard screen and its 70Wh battery. Tom's Guide expects less with the OLED screen. One shopping guide estimates 16 to 17 hours on the standard screen and 12 to 13 hours with OLED. Treat that as a rough guess.

Lenovo cites more than 13 hours for the ThinkPad in its own test. These numbers come from light-use tests. Video calls, bright screens and heavy work will lower them.

08

Screens in more detail

In short: Both offer a sharp standard screen and an OLED upgrade. On the XPS, the standard screen is actually brighter and lasts longer on battery.

Read the details on screens

The XPS 14 starts with a 14-inch screen at 1920x1200. It is not a touch screen. It runs from 1 to 120Hz and reaches 500 nits.

The OLED option is a touch screen at 2880x1800. It runs from 20 to 120Hz and reaches 400 nits. Tom's Guide calls it a tandem OLED panel. Dell listed the OLED upgrade at $150 in spring 2026.

So OLED brings richer colour and deeper blacks. But on the XPS it is not brighter, and it costs battery life.

The ThinkPad follows a similar pattern. It has a 1920x1200 IPS base screen and a 2.8K OLED option at up to 120Hz. US retailers listed both, and some touch versions too.

For most work, the standard screen is sharp enough. Choose OLED if you edit photos or video, or watch a lot of video. Skip it if battery life matters most.

09

Business features and support

In short: The ThinkPad is built with IT departments in mind. The XPS is aimed more at individual buyers.

Read the details on business features

Lenovo offers the X1 Carbon with Intel's vPro platform. It adds remote management and security tools. IT departments rely on these to set up, monitor and secure laptops across a whole company.

ThinkPads have also long been tested against MIL-SPEC standards. These are military testing procedures covering drops, dust, vibration and temperature extremes. Passing them does not mean a laptop is indestructible.

The XPS line has traditionally been aimed at individual buyers, rather than large company fleets. Dell's Dell Pro lines exist for buyers who need business management. A company shopping for that would likely look past the XPS.

One reviewer at The Gadgeteer called the Gen 14 the best business ultraportable you can buy in 2026. That is one opinion. LaptopMedia is more careful. It says the laptop suits people who value portability, build quality and repairability over performance per dollar.

10

Which setup to buy

In short: Memory is soldered, so pay for enough at the start. Add stronger graphics only if you need them. Skip OLED if battery life matters most.

Read the buying advice

This is our advice, based on the specs and prices above. It is not a lab result.

For office work, study and browsing

The base chip is enough. Take 16GB on the XPS, or the 32GB that comes with the ThinkPad. Keep the standard screen for longer battery life.

For photo and video editing

Pick 32GB of memory. Dell charged about $450 for that step in spring 2026. Consider OLED for richer colour. Remember the battery cost.

For light gaming or graphics work

On the XPS, take a Core Ultra X chip for the stronger Arc graphics. On either laptop, do not expect a gaming laptop's speed.

For business or a company fleet

Choose the ThinkPad with vPro. On the Dell, Windows 11 Pro was a $60 option in spring 2026. Ask your IT team before you buy.

Two mistakes to avoid

  • Do not buy too little memory. It is soldered on both laptops, so you cannot add more later.
  • Do not judge by starting price. Price the exact setup you want on both sites, because prices changed a lot this year.

Add-on prices come from Dell's store page in spring 2026. Dell raised prices in May, so today's figures may be higher.

Who tends to prefer which

People who tend to prefer the XPS 14

  • Individual buyers who want a lower entry price
  • Anyone who likes a minimal, MacBook-like design
  • People who mostly use the laptop at a desk
  • Buyers who want an optional OLED touch screen

People who tend to prefer the ThinkPad

  • Anyone who carries a laptop daily and feels every gram
  • Typists who want the strongest keyboard feel
  • Businesses that need vPro or remote management
  • Buyers who want more ports without an adapter

Price used to favour the Dell by a wide margin. In 2026 that gap has shrunk. Match the memory and screen, and the two cost about the same. So decide on weight, keyboard, ports and support, not on price alone.

13

Buying in Uzbekistan

Every price above is a US price, so expect your local shop to differ. Memory costs have pushed prices up everywhere this year. Working through the checklist below can spare you an expensive mistake.

  • Ask for today's price in writing. Laptop prices moved several times this year. A quote from last month may already be out of date.
  • Check the exact model name. Some 2025 stock still carries the old Dell Premium name. It is an older design.
  • Confirm the warranty in writing. An official seller usually backs the laptop with local service. A laptop brought in unofficially can be cheaper up front and far costlier the day it breaks.
  • Decide on memory before you buy. Both laptops solder their memory. You cannot add more later.
  • Plan for adapters. The XPS has only USB-C ports. Budget for a dock or adapter if you use HDMI or older accessories.
FAQ

Questions people ask

Why did Dell bring the XPS name back?

The 2025 rebrand to Dell, Dell Plus and Dell Premium was widely criticised. Dell vice chairman Jeff Clarke apologised at the January 2026 launch. XPS returned as the premium consumer brand.

Is the old "Dell Premium" the same as the new XPS?

No. Dell Premium was a 2025 name for the previous-generation laptop. Dell later renamed those models back to XPS. The 2026 XPS 14 and XPS 16 are redesigned models.

Which is cheaper, the XPS or the ThinkPad?

The entry XPS is cheaper, but it has 16GB of memory and a smaller chip. With similar memory and an OLED screen, the two cost about the same in the prices we found.

Why did the XPS 14 price change so often?

Dell first raised the launch price, then cut it in February, then raised it again in May. Dell pointed to component costs. Memory and storage chips got much more expensive in 2026.

Is last year's XPS 14 worth buying?

It can be. In August 2026, Windows Central saw it with 32GB, 1TB and a 3.2K OLED screen for $1,549.99. It has older chips and a heavier design. Check the exact specs first.

Can I upgrade the memory or storage later?

Memory is soldered on both laptops, so it cannot be upgraded. The XPS uses a standard M.2 drive for storage. Lenovo designed the Gen 14 to be easier to open.

Which one is better for gaming?

Neither is built for serious gaming. See our guide to ASUS, MSI and Lenovo Legion gaming laptops.
