1. Two Different Approaches to Mid-Range Phones

The mid-range phone market has gotten really competitive - people expect solid performance even at a lower price. Here we compare the Samsung Galaxy A54 and iPhone SE 2024, two very different takes on the sub-$500 category.

Samsung's approach is to bring flagship-feeling features down to a lower price - a fast AMOLED screen and multiple camera lenses that make the A54 feel like a much pricier phone at first glance.

Apple goes the opposite way, putting its flagship processor into a familiar, compact design instead of chasing new screen tech or extra cameras. This appeals to people who care more about raw speed and pocketability than screen size or camera versatility.

3. Display Quality

The screen is how you interact with your phone the most. The Galaxy A54 has a 6.4-inch Super AMOLED display running at 120Hz, so scrolling and animations feel very smooth. Since it's AMOLED, blacks are deep and contrast is excellent, making it great for watching video, gaming, and using outdoors thanks to its high brightness.

Samsung's screen is clearly bigger and smoother than the iPhone SE's more dated display.

The iPhone SE 2024 uses a smaller 4.7-inch LCD screen at a standard 60Hz. It has True Tone, which adjusts color based on the lighting around you, but the hardware itself is older - thick bezels limit usable screen space, which can make reading or multitasking feel cramped. That said, the smaller size makes it very easy to use one-handed, which some people genuinely prefer.

4. Processing Power

While Samsung wins on display, Apple clearly wins on raw speed. The iPhone SE 2024 runs the same A16 Bionic chip found in Apple's flagship phones, which comfortably outperforms the A54's Exynos 1380 chip. Whether you're editing video, gaming, or juggling multiple apps, the iPhone SE has noticeably more headroom.

The Exynos 1380 in the A54 is well-tuned for everyday use - smooth for browsing, messaging, and casual games. It can occasionally stutter under heavy multitasking or intense graphics, but for typical daily use it holds up just fine.

5. Battery Life

Battery capacity is limited by phone size, and that creates a clear gap here. The Galaxy A54 fits a much larger 5000mAh battery, while the compact iPhone SE only has room for 2018mAh.

In our mixed-use testing - browsing, streaming, messaging - the Galaxy A54 easily lasted a full day, often stretching into a second day on one charge. Its combination of a big battery and efficient chip makes it reliable for long days away from a charger.

The iPhone SE relies on iOS efficiency and the A16 chip to get the most out of its smaller battery. For moderate use - emails, calls, occasional music - it'll get you through a full day. But heavy gaming or video recording will drain it noticeably faster, so power users may need a top-up mid-day.

6. Camera Comparison

The Galaxy A54 has a versatile triple-camera setup: a 50MP main sensor with stabilization, a 12MP ultra-wide for landscapes, and a 5MP macro lens for close-ups. That gives you flexibility to shoot different types of scenes.

The iPhone SE 2024 keeps it simple with just one 12MP wide camera - no ultra-wide or macro lens. Instead, it leans on Apple's computational photography, using Smart HDR and Deep Fusion to balance exposure and color automatically. In good daylight, it actually produces very natural, accurate photos that hold up well against the A54.

Samsung A54 Profile

  • Big, smooth 120Hz AMOLED display.
  • Large 5000mAh battery for multi-day use.
  • Versatile triple-camera setup with ultra-wide.
  • Modern, thin-bezel design.

System Trade-offs

  • The chip can struggle a bit under heavy load.
  • A bit heavier and bulkier in hand.
  • Shorter software update window than iPhone.

iPhone SE 2024 Profile

  • Fast A16 Bionic chip - flagship-level performance.
  • Compact and easy to use one-handed.
  • Years of guaranteed software updates.
  • Solid aluminum build quality.

System Trade-offs

  • Smaller, older LCD screen with thick bezels.
  • Small battery means more frequent charging.
  • No ultra-wide camera or optical zoom.

7. Software and Ecosystem

Choosing between these often comes down to which ecosystem you're already in. The Galaxy A54 runs Android with Samsung's One UI on top, giving you a lot of customization - themes, home screen layout, file management. It also connects nicely with Windows PCs and other Samsung smart home devices.

The iPhone SE 2024 is an affordable way into Apple's ecosystem - it works seamlessly with iPads, MacBooks, Apple Watch, and AirPods through AirDrop and iCloud. Apple also supports its phones with updates for years, which means the SE will keep getting new features and security patches for a long time.

Definitive Structural Evaluation & Verdict

Overall, these two phones serve pretty different users. The Galaxy A54 is the better pick if you want a big, beautiful screen, strong battery life, and camera flexibility without paying flagship prices. The iPhone SE 2024 is the better pick if you want flagship-level speed, years of software support, and a phone that fits comfortably in a smaller pocket.
