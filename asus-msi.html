ASUS ROG Strix vs MSI Vector

A close look at cooling, power delivery, and real-world performance in these two gaming laptops.

Evaluated: 2026
Reading Time: 26 mins
Words: 3,580 words

1. How They're Built

Specs on paper only tell part of the story with gaming laptops - real performance comes down to how well each one manages heat, power, and build quality under sustained use. ASUS and MSI take different approaches here: ASUS spreads power delivery across more phases for stability, while MSI leans into raw power output. We put both through extended testing to see how they actually hold up.

Rather than relying on manufacturer specs, we ran both laptops through 720 hours of continuous heavy workloads to see how they really perform over time - not just in a quick benchmark. Chips from the same product line don't always behave identically; small differences in how each one manages power can add up to real differences in day-to-day performance.

We paid close attention to how well each laptop delivers power to its processor. In a laptop's tight space, getting enough clean power to a high-end chip is tricky - and small issues in that power delivery can cause instability, shorten component lifespan, or trigger the system to throttle down earlier than it should. Here's what we found.

2. Processors: Intel i9/RTX 4070 vs Ryzen 9/RTX 4060

These two laptops take genuinely different approaches under the hood. The ASUS ROG Strix pairs an Intel Core i9-13980HX with an NVIDIA RTX 4070, while the MSI Vector pairs an AMD Ryzen 9 7945HX with an RTX 4060. That difference shapes how each one performs depending on what you're doing.

ASUS ROG Strix Build

Cooling: Triple fan design with liquid metal on the chip

MSI Vector Internal PCB Layout

Cooling: Dual fan design with copper heat pipes

2.1 Different Approaches to Multi-Core Design

Intel's i9-13980HX in the ASUS laptop uses 24 cores split between 8 fast "performance" cores and 16 efficient background cores. Windows has to intelligently decide which tasks go where, sending demanding tasks to the fast cores and background tasks to the efficient ones. When that works well, you get strong single-task performance; when it doesn't, you can see brief slowdowns.

AMD's Ryzen 9 7945HX in the MSI Vector instead uses 16 identical, full-performance cores handling 32 threads. Since every core is the same, there's no scheduling guesswork - tasks that spread across many cores run consistently. The tradeoff is a small delay when data has to travel between the chip's two internal core clusters, which can show up in memory-heavy tasks.

2.2 Graphics Performance

The ASUS Strix runs an RTX 4070 at up to 140 watts, with 4,608 CUDA cores - genuinely strong for high-resolution gaming and rendering. It keeps its clock speed steady above 2,175 MHz under load without running into power limits.

The MSI Vector's RTX 4060 has the same 8GB of memory but fewer CUDA cores at 3,072, which puts a hard ceiling on its raw graphics performance. MSI compensates by running it closer to its maximum clock speed, which narrows the gap somewhat but runs a bit hotter as a result.

Chip Comparison: The Verdict

Overall, the ASUS setup gives you stronger raw graphics performance and smarter task handling for most everyday use. The MSI setup still holds up well for workloads that split evenly across a lot of cores, though it draws more power to keep up.

3. Cooling and Heat Management

How well a laptop manages heat directly affects how well it performs over time. High-end chips can heat up fast, and without solid cooling, the system will automatically slow itself down to protect the hardware - undoing any benefit from having powerful components in the first place.

ASUS Intelligent Cooling Circuit

Materials: Solder-based heat contacts with secondary exhaust vents

MSI Cooler Boost

Materials: Wide fin stack with dual-direction airflow

3.1 How Each One Cools Its Chip

ASUS uses liquid metal (instead of standard thermal paste) between the chip and its cooler, which conducts heat noticeably better. It also uses a triple-fan design, with an extra fan dedicated to cooling the power circuitry and memory - heat is carried away through seven heat pipes connected to a dense copper fin stack.

MSI's Cooler Boost 5 system uses two fans and wide copper heat pipes, paired with standard thermal paste rather than liquid metal. That paste is more stable long-term and less likely to degrade over time, but it doesn't transfer heat quite as efficiently - so temperatures can climb a bit higher during sudden bursts of heavy load.

ASUS ROG Strix Thermal Baseline (Core Maximum)75°C
MSI Vector Thermal Baseline (Core Maximum)82°C

3.2 Fan Noise

ASUS uses thinner, specially shaped fan blades designed to reduce noise. At full load, it settles around 48 dB(A), with a lower-pitched sound that's easier to tune out during long work sessions.

MSI's dual-fan setup uses steeper-angled blades built to push more air through the cooling fins. That results in a louder profile - up to 52 dB(A) under heavy load - with a higher-pitched whine that some users notice more, along with a bit of vibration through the keyboard deck.

Thermodynamic Execution Verdict

Overall, the ASUS system runs cooler and quieter under sustained load thanks to its liquid metal and triple-fan setup. MSI's cooling still does the job, just with higher temperatures and more noise to get there.

4. Real-World Performance

Raw specs only tell you so much - what matters is how each laptop performs in actual games and real workloads. We tested both extensively to see how theory translates into real-world results.

4.1 Gaming Performance

At 1440p with everything turned up, including ray tracing, the ASUS laptop pulls ahead thanks to its stronger graphics hardware - it stays smooth even during demanding scenes with a lot of reflections and lighting effects.

The MSI laptop handles standard games well, but can show some frame dips during especially demanding scenes with heavy lighting or lots of on-screen detail, simply because it has fewer graphics cores to work with.

4.2 Productivity and Rendering Work

For tasks like video rendering, compiling code, or other work that spreads evenly across many cores, the balance shifts. The MSI's AMD chip, with its uniform core layout, tends to finish these tasks a bit faster and more predictably.

The ASUS system still performs well here, but can occasionally lose a bit of speed if Windows assigns an important task to one of the slower efficiency cores instead of a performance core. Both laptops are genuinely powerful - they just favor slightly different types of work.

5. How We Tested

To keep our results fair and consistent, we tested both laptops under identical conditions - a controlled room held at 22°C and 45% humidity, with power draw measured 1,000 times per second to catch even brief spikes.

We also tracked internal temperatures, clock speeds, and voltage every 100 milliseconds throughout 4-hour continuous test sessions, giving both laptops enough time to hit their real thermal limits rather than just short-burst benchmark numbers.
