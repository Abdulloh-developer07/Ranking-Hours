iPhone 18 Pro Max or Galaxy S26 Ultra? Spend a day with each

Spec tables tell you which phone has the bigger number. A normal day tells you which one you would actually be happier holding. So we followed one, from the morning alarm to the last scroll before sleep.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max went on sale on 18 September, and the Galaxy S26 Ultra has been in shops since March. That makes this the first fair fight between Apple's newest big phone and Samsung's current one. Both have 6.9-inch screens. Both start at about $1,300 in the United States. Both will be the most expensive thing in your pocket by a distance.

They also disagree about almost everything else, and the disagreements are more interesting than the similarities. Apple has gone for a faster chip, a longer-lasting battery and a camera that changes its aperture. Samsung has gone for a lighter body, a built-in stylus, a huge 200-megapixel main sensor and a screen you can hide from the person next to you on the bus.

How we compiled this Everything here comes from manufacturer specifications, retailer listings and published reviews and tests from March to September 2026. We have not run our own measurements. Where two sources disagree, we say so. Prices are US list prices and will differ in Uzbekistan.

One more piece of context. Apple raised the starting price of its Pro models by $100 this year, and industry coverage ties that rise to the high cost of memory chips. The Galaxy has been on sale since March, and sale prices often appear a few months after launch. Keep that in mind when we reach the money question at the end.

06:30

The alarm, and the first look at the screen

Even

The first thing either phone does in the morning is be looked at, and here the two are closer than the marketing suggests. Both use a 6.9-inch OLED panel running up to 120 Hz. Samsung's is a touch sharper on paper, at 3,120 by 1,440 pixels against Apple's 2,868 by 1,320, but at this size nobody will see the difference with the naked eye.

Where they do differ is peak brightness. Apple rates the iPhone for up to 3,000 nits outdoors, while Samsung lists roughly 2,600. On a bright morning that gap is small and only matters at the extreme end, but if you live somewhere with hard midday sun, the iPhone has a small edge.

Unlocking is a matter of habit. The iPhone uses Face ID, which works without touching anything, and Apple has shrunk the Dynamic Island a little this year. The Galaxy uses an ultrasonic fingerprint reader under the glass. Face ID is quicker when your hands are full, and the fingerprint reader is more convenient in the dark, when you do not want to hold the phone up to your face, or when you are wearing a mask or a scarf.

Samsung has one screen feature Apple lacks, and it deserves a mention. Privacy Display narrows the viewing angle so that someone sitting beside you sees a dim, washed-out image. If you often read messages on a crowded train or in a shared taxi, that is not a gimmick. It is a real, daily benefit.

08:15

The commute, and what it feels like in one hand

Edge: Galaxy

This is where the Samsung starts to pull ahead, and it comes down to a single number. The Galaxy S26 Ultra weighs 214 grams. The iPhone 18 Pro Max weighs 249. That is a difference of 35 grams, about the weight of a few coins. It sounds small, but a phone is something you hold for hours, often above your face, and reviewers who have handled both keep coming back to it.

The Samsung is also thinner, at 7.9 millimetres against 8.75. Add a case to each and the gap changes little, but the Galaxy still feels less like a brick. Apple gives you an aluminium frame with a glass back, and Samsung an all-glass back, and the iPhone has slightly rounder corners. Those choices affect grip, but the weight matters more.

If you type with one hand or read standing up, the difference is real. If you keep the phone on a desk or in a car mount most of the day, you will not care.

SIM cards and travel

One practical detail belongs in the commute. Apple sells eSIM-only versions of the iPhone 18 Pro Max in several markets, including the United States, Canada, Japan, Mexico and the UAE. Samsung still offers a physical SIM tray. If you swap SIM cards when you travel, or if your mobile operator in Uzbekistan does not yet support eSIM well, this matters more than any camera spec. Check which version you are buying before you pay.

10:00

At work: notes, email and getting things done

Depends on you

Ask people who own a big flagship why they bought it and half will say the screen, for reading and typing. Both phones do the job well, but they push you toward different habits.

The Galaxy S26 Ultra still has the S Pen, tucked into the body. If you write notes by hand, mark up documents or sign forms, nothing on the iPhone side comes close, and few other phones offer it. It is a niche feature, but for the people who use it, it is the reason to buy.

The iPhone's advantage is that it plays best with everything else Apple makes. If you use a Mac or an iPad, copying on one device and pasting on another, or taking a call from the laptop, just works. Our guide to the Apple and Samsung ecosystems goes through that in detail, and it is worth reading before you commit to either.

Software is the other difference. The iPhone launches with iOS 27, whose headline feature is a rebuilt assistant called Siri AI. Reports say the more capable parts arrive in stages and by language, and it is not available in the European Union at launch. The Galaxy runs Android 16 with One UI 8.5, and the beta of One UI 9, based on Android 17, is already out, so an update is likely to come soon. Samsung also lets you change more: layouts, split-screen windows, and a desktop-style mode when you plug into a monitor. iOS is tidier and asks less of you.

13:00

Lunchtime photos and quick video

Different strengths

Cameras are the hardest thing to compare from a desk, because the best one depends on how you shoot. What we can do is describe how each is built, so that you know what to look for.

Apple has kept a triple 48-megapixel setup on the back, with a main camera, an ultrawide and a 4x periscope telephoto. The new part is the main camera, which now changes its aperture between f/1.4, f/1.8, f/2.8 and f/4.0 depending on the scene. In dim light it opens wide to let in more, and in bright light or for close portraits it closes down for more depth. It is a hardware trick that phones rarely attempt, and in theory it should help both low-light shots and natural background blur.

Samsung leads with numbers. The S26 Ultra has a 200-megapixel main sensor and a set of dedicated zoom lenses, which is why it tends to be the pick for people who care about zooming into distant subjects. Megapixels alone do not make a better picture, but a large sensor with strong processing gives you the freedom to crop after the fact, which many enthusiasts value.

Video is another split. Apple has a long-standing reputation for smooth, consistent video, and it is reasonable to expect the same here. Samsung's video is very capable too, but if you shoot a lot of clips of your children or travel, the iPhone tends to give more reliable results with little effort. For selfies, the iPhone's front camera is listed at 18 megapixels against 12 on the Galaxy, though you should judge that on results and not on the number.

Choose the iPhone for reliable video and low-light photos. Choose the Galaxy for zoom, cropping and a bigger toolbox.
15:00

Mid-afternoon: calls, drops and the cost of breaking one

Even

Somewhere between lunch and dinner, most phones get a small crisis. A call in a noisy street, a slip off a table, a glass of tea a bit too close. None of it shows up on a spec sheet, so it is worth thinking through before you spend this much.

On paper the two are matched on durability. Both are rated IP68 for dust and water, which covers rain, spills and a quick dip. The front glass differs in name: Apple uses its own Ceramic Shield, and spec listings show Samsung using Corning's Gorilla Glass Armor 2. Both companies claim improved scratch resistance over earlier generations, and neither is easy to fault. Neither glass makes a phone drop-proof, and a hard fall onto a corner can crack either one.

Weight plays a small part here. A heavier phone hits the floor with more energy, and a lighter one is a little easier to keep hold of in the first place, so it is not a clear win for either side. What does make a difference is the case. A good case and a screen protector cost a fraction of a repair, and on phones this large they are the single best bit of insurance you can buy. Apple's MagSafe range gives iPhone owners a lot of choice in cases with magnets. Samsung owners have plenty of cases too, and the S Pen slot stays clear of most of them.

What to check for calls and sound

We cannot tell you from a desk which phone has the better speakers or microphones, and we would be wary of any review that claims a clear winner without hearing both in your environment. What we can suggest is a simple test at the shop: make a call in a noisy place, play a song at full volume, and record a short video in a busy spot to hear how each handles wind and crowds. Ten minutes of that tells you more than a table of decibels.

If it does break

Screens on 6.9-inch flagships are expensive to replace, and a repair can cost a real share of what a mid-range phone would. Both Apple and Samsung sell extended protection plans in many countries, and it is worth asking whether they are offered in Uzbekistan and what they actually cover. If they are not, weigh the cost of a case and a good protector more heavily, and ask a local repair shop what a screen swap costs for each model. It is a dull question, but it can change which phone is cheaper to own.

17:30

Games and video on the way home

Edge: iPhone

The iPhone 18 Pro Max runs Apple's new A20 Pro chip, built on a 2-nanometre process, with a vapour-chamber system to keep it cool. Early Geekbench 6 results put it around 4,700 for single-core and 12,400 for multi-core work, which is very high.

The Galaxy uses a version of the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, tuned for Samsung, made on a 3-nanometre process, and paired with up to 16 GB of memory. Samsung says it improves on last year's chip by about 19 percent for the processor, 24 percent for graphics and 39 percent for AI tasks. It is an extremely quick phone, and for almost everything you will do, it will not feel slow.

The difference shows up in long gaming sessions. One outlet's test of a demanding game at 60 frames per second found the iPhone averaging about 58 frames and the Galaxy about 51. That is a single test, and other games will give different results, but it points the same way as the performance scores: the A20 Pro holds up better under sustained load. The same outlet found the Samsung ahead in some AI tasks, which fits the extra memory and the attention Samsung has paid to on-device AI.

If you play for ten minutes on a train, either phone will do. If you play demanding games for an hour at a time, the iPhone will stay cooler and steadier.

19:30

Evening: battery, charging and what is left in the tank

Edge: iPhone

Both phones have batteries of around 5,000 mAh, so on paper they are equals. In practice they are not. In a 5G battery test reported by Tom's Guide, the iPhone 18 Pro Max lasted an average of 18 hours and 27 minutes, against 16 hours and 40 minutes for the Galaxy S26 Ultra in its standard display mode. That is a gap of nearly two hours, and it is what you would expect from a more efficient chip.

Apple also rates its phone for up to 45 hours of video playback in the eSIM-only version and 43 hours in the version with a SIM tray, so the battery life you get depends on which model you buy. Real-world use will always differ, since brightness, signal strength, gaming and background apps all change the result.

Charging is much closer. Neither phone will surprise you here, and both support fast wired charging and wireless charging. If you charge overnight, you will not notice any difference at all. If you tend to run a phone down to the last few percent each evening, the extra two hours on the iPhone is the difference between reaching home and reaching for a charger.

23:00

Late at night: money, storage and the long game

Even, with caveats

The last question of the day is the one that decides most purchases. The iPhone 18 Pro Max starts at $1,299 with 256 GB, and Apple has added a 2 TB version for the first time, at $2,499. The Galaxy S26 Ultra starts at roughly $1,300 for 256 GB, and goes up from there. At the starting tier, the two cost almost exactly the same, and sales on the Galaxy can lower the price in a few months.

Software support is where both are strong. Samsung has extended its update promise to seven years on its recent flagships, and Apple's phones have long been supported for seven years or more, with iOS 27 running on the iPhone 11 from 2019. Neither is a risk on this front.

Resale value is where they diverge. Used iPhones tend to keep more of their price than used Galaxies, and that matters if you like to sell your phone after two or three years. If you plan to keep it until it breaks, resale hardly matters, and the difference is the price you pay on day one.

If you do not need a phone this large, remember there are smaller siblings. Apple's iPhone 18 Pro has a 6.3-inch screen and shares the same chip, and Samsung's standard Galaxy S26 is also much more compact. And if your budget is closer to $600 than $1,300, our comparison of the Galaxy A57 and iPhone 17e covers that end of the market.

So which one fits your routine?

After a full day, the split is clearer than any table makes it. The iPhone 18 Pro Max is the stronger performer: faster, longer-lasting and more reliable for video. The Galaxy S26 Ultra is the more distinctive tool: lighter, more customisable, with a stylus, a privacy screen and a lot of zoom.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max suits you if

  • You use a Mac, an iPad or an Apple Watch
  • You play demanding games or record a lot of video
  • You want the longest battery life on the market
  • You sell your phone after a couple of years
  • You would rather have simple software that stays out of the way

The Galaxy S26 Ultra suits you if

  • You write or draw on the screen, or sign documents
  • You want a lighter phone for long one-handed use
  • You zoom and crop your photos a lot
  • You ride crowded buses or trains and value Privacy Display
  • You want a physical SIM and more control over the software

If none of that decides it, use the tiebreaker that matters more than any spec: what does the rest of your life run on? A watch, a laptop and a family group chat will pull you towards one side more strongly than a camera sensor ever will.

How to think about storage and timing

Storage is the quiet part of the price. Both phones start at 256 GB, which is plenty for most people if photos back up to the cloud. Moving up to 512 GB adds around $200 on each side, so only do it if you record a lot of 4K video or keep large offline libraries. The very large tiers, such as Apple's new 2 TB option, are for a small group of heavy users. For everyone else they are simply money that could have gone into a case, a charger and a warranty.

Timing matters too. Apple's phones rarely see big discounts in their first months, while Samsung's often drop within a season. If you can wait until the winter sales, the Galaxy tends to become the more affordable of the two. If you want the newest model on day one, the iPhone is here now and the Galaxy is six months old, and Samsung has usually launched its next flagship in the first months of the year, so you would be paying a premium for a phone that is about to be succeeded. Whichever you choose, avoid buying in the first hours of a new launch, when supply is short and prices are at their highest.

Before you buy in Uzbekistan

  • Check the SIM setup. Some iPhone 18 Pro Max versions are eSIM-only. Ask which version the shop is selling, and confirm your operator supports eSIM.
  • Buy with a local warranty. An official seller gives you repair options nearby. A grey import may save money at first and cost more later.
  • Look at the model number on the box. Regional versions of the same phone can differ in battery rating, radios and included accessories.
  • Compare the total price. Add a case, a screen protector and a fast charger. On a phone this size, those extras are not small.

Questions people ask

Is the iPhone 18 Pro Max worth the extra money over the iPhone 17 Pro Max?

That depends on what you have now. Apple raised the starting price by $100, and the biggest gains are the faster chip, the variable-aperture camera and better battery life. If you already own a recent Pro Max, the jump may be modest.

Does the Galaxy S26 Ultra really have a better zoom?

Samsung builds its phone around dedicated zoom hardware and a 200 MP main sensor, so it is strong when you zoom or crop. Apple's 4x periscope is very good too. The right answer depends on the distance and the light, so look at sample photos taken in conditions like yours.

Which phone lasts longer on a charge?

In the published 5G test we cite, the iPhone lasted nearly two hours longer. Your own result will depend on brightness, signal and how much you game.

Do I need the S Pen?

Only if you write, draw or annotate. Many owners never take it out, and if that sounds like you, the iPhone's advantages may count for more.

Should I wait for the standard iPhone 18?

Only if a Pro model is more than you need. The standard iPhone 18 was not part of the September launch, and reports point to spring 2027. If you want a big flagship today, you have no reason to wait.
