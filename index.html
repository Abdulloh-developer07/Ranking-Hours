Why this site exists

Most "vs" articles online read like two spec sheets pasted next to each other. That tells you a processor is faster or a screen is a few nits brighter, but it doesn't tell you which one you'd actually be happier with in six months. Ranking Hours started as a way to close that gap — fewer raw numbers up front, more attention to the stuff that shows up after the unboxing: how a laptop's fan behaves under a real workload, whether a phone's camera app is fast enough to catch a kid mid-laugh, whether a car's infotainment system is still annoying after week three.

We don't run a lab with a dozen engineers, and we're upfront about that. What we do is read the manufacturer documentation closely, cross-check it against user reports and professional reviews, and think through how a difference on paper plays out in daily use. When two products are genuinely close, we say so, instead of forcing a winner for the sake of a tidy headline.

How a comparison actually gets written

Every page starts with the official specifications and pricing for each product, pulled from the manufacturer's own listings. From there we read through independent reviews and long-term user feedback to see where the spec sheet and the lived experience start to disagree — that gap is usually the most useful part of any comparison. Pages get revisited when a product is updated, discontinued, or replaced, so a comparison you read today should still reflect what's actually on shelves.

Before you decide

Prices, software versions and even hardware revisions change faster than any article can track in real time. Treat what's on this site as a starting point for narrowing your options, then check current pricing and the exact model number with the retailer before buying. If a comparison here doesn't match what you're seeing in a store, the newer listing is almost always the one to trust — let us know and we'll take a look.
