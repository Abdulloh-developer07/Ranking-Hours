Corrections Policy

How we handle mistakes, fact-check our reviews, and keep our data accurate over time.

Status: Active & Verified

Our Commitment to Accuracy

We're committed to keeping every review, benchmark, and comparison on Ranking Hours accurate and up to date. We follow the same principles as the International Fact-Checking Network, and we keep a public log of any corrections we make so readers can trust what they're reading.

1. Who We Are

Ranking Hours (ranking-hours.uz) is an independent tech review and comparison site covering phones, laptops, and other consumer electronics. Because firmware updates, benchmarks, and hardware specs change quickly, our data can go out of date fast - which is exactly why we maintain this corrections policy.

Getting things right isn't just a formality to us - it's something we take seriously for our readers, the manufacturers we cover, and our own credibility. This page explains how we find, review, and fix mistakes, whether that's a wrong number, a typo, or an incomplete review. No advertiser or partner gets a say in how we handle corrections - our data stays independent.

We believe accountability should be clear and easy to track. When we find an error, we follow a consistent process to fix it - always prioritizing accuracy over convenience.

2. How We Fact-Check Before Publishing

Before any review or benchmark goes live, it goes through a careful review process designed to catch mistakes and avoid bias before publication.

We Only Use Verified Sources

We don't use rumors, social media hearsay, or unverified leaks in our published rankings. We rely on official manufacturer documentation, verified benchmark results (like Geekbench, AnTuTu, or 3DMark), and our own hands-on lab testing. Any speculative or pre-release info is clearly marked as a "Theoretical Forecast" so readers know it isn't confirmed.

A Second Set of Eyes on Everything

Every comparison, calculation, or performance chart gets reviewed by at least two of our editors before it's published, checking the numbers against our actual test logs. This second review catches mistakes and keeps any one person's bias from skewing a result.

3. How We Categorize Errors

When we find or get reported an error, we sort it into one of three categories based on how serious it is, so we know how quickly to fix it.

Minor Level Corrections

Minor: typos, spelling mistakes, or small formatting issues that don't affect the actual information.

Resolution: Within 48 Hours Max

Moderate Level Corrections

Factual: wrong specs, battery capacities, prices, or connectivity details.

Resolution: Within 24 Hours Max

Major Level Corrections

Major: calculation errors or bad benchmark data that actually skews a comparison and could lead someone to a bad purchase decision.

Resolution: Within 6 Hours Max

4. How We Fix Errors

Once we confirm an error after publishing, we follow the same process every time:

Stage 1: Signal Ingestion, Triage, and Ticket Provisioning

The moment we get a report or spot an issue ourselves, we log it and flag the page for review.

Stage 2: Technical Auditing and Primary Re-Verification

A staff editor checks the original source or re-verifies with the manufacturer to confirm whether the error is real.

Stage 3: Core Database Refactoring and Cache Flashing

Once confirmed, we update the number or fact directly on the page, and the fix goes live right away.

Stage 4: Mandatory Public Disclaimers and Editorial Transparency

We add a visible note to the page showing what the original mistake was, what it's been corrected to, and when.

Common Questions

Do you fix errors on old articles? Yes. No matter how old a page is, if we find a factual error, we fix it and log it in our public corrections registry.

Can a correction note be removed later? No. Once a correction note is added, it stays on the page permanently as part of its history - no exceptions, regardless of any partnership or sponsorship.

What if a product's performance changes after a software update? In that case, we don't treat it as a correction - we add an "Optimization Update" note instead. This keeps our original test intact while showing readers the latest performance data.
