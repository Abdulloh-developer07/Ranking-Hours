ASUS ROG, MSI or Lenovo Legion: which gaming laptop is right for you

Three brands, dozens of models, and prices from about $1,000 to $6,000. This guide explains it in plain words, with real prices, and a tool that matches a laptop to your budget.

01

The basics, in plain words

A gaming laptop has two parts that matter more than anything else. The graphics chip, called the GPU, decides how smooth and sharp your games look. The cooling system decides how long the laptop can run at full speed before it slows down.

Everything else, like the brand, the RGB lights, the keyboard, is nice to have. It does not change how well the laptop plays games. So this guide focuses on the GPU first, then on how each brand builds around it.

Three brands come up again and again when people search for a gaming laptop: ASUS ROG, MSI, and Lenovo Legion. All three now sell laptops with Nvidia's newest chips, called the RTX 50 series. All three also sell a cheap starter model and a very expensive flagship model. This guide covers both ends for each brand.

Words explained
  • GPU: the graphics chip. It draws the picture in a game. A stronger GPU means smoother, sharper games.
  • CPU: the main processor. It handles everything else the computer does. Most gaming laptops now have a strong enough CPU, so the GPU matters more.
  • VRAM: the GPU's own memory. More VRAM lets a game use bigger, sharper textures.
  • Refresh rate (Hz): how many times the screen updates each second. A higher number looks smoother, especially in fast games.
  • DLSS: a smart feature from Nvidia. It lets a game run at a lower detail level. Then it sharpens the picture with AI. The result looks nearly as good, but runs faster.
02

Graphics chips: the part that matters most

In short: the GPU name tells you what a laptop can do. RTX 5060 is good for 1080p. RTX 5070 and 5070 Ti handle 1440p. RTX 5080 and 5090 are for 4K and the heaviest games.

Read the details on each GPU tier

Nvidia sells five main laptop GPUs in the RTX 50 series. Each one is built for a different kind of gaming. Here is what each one is actually good for, from the cheapest to the most expensive.

RTX 50601080p, smooth and affordable
RTX 50701440p, the mainstream choice
RTX 5070 Ti1440p with room to spare
RTX 50804K and heavy ray tracing
RTX 5090the fastest option available

The RTX 5060 has 8GB of its own memory and uses up to 80 watts of power. It handles 1080p games at high settings easily, and it can even do 1440p if you turn on DLSS, the AI sharpening feature. It is the GPU found in most laptops under $1,500.

The RTX 5070 also has 8GB of memory, but it uses more power, up to 115 watts, so it runs faster. Reviewers call it the mainstream sweet spot. It plays 1440p games well and can manage 4K with DLSS turned on.

The RTX 5070 Ti steps up to 12GB of memory and up to 150 watts. The extra memory helps in newer games that need more of it, and it gives noticeably better 4K performance than the plain 5070.

The RTX 5080 has 16GB of memory and up to 175 watts. It is built for people who want the most performance and are willing to pay for it. It handles 4K gaming and heavy ray tracing, which is realistic lighting and reflections, without much trouble.

The RTX 5090 is the fastest laptop GPU you can buy right now. It has 24GB of memory. Reviewers who tested it against the RTX 5080 found that the 5090 is faster, but often not by as much as the price difference suggests. Many reviewers actually recommend the 5080 as the smarter buy, and say the 5090 makes sense only if you truly need the extra power, for example for 4K gaming or heavy creative work.

One thing that matters more than the name

The same GPU name can perform differently in two different laptops. This is because of something called TGP, or how many watts a laptop lets the chip use. A thin, light laptop often limits its RTX 5070 Ti to save on heat and battery. A thick, heavy laptop can let the same chip run at full power. So a bigger, less portable laptop is often faster than a slim one with the same GPU name. Always check the wattage of the GPU, not just its name, if top speed matters to you.

Part 2The three brandsWhat each one gets right, and what to watch for
04

ASUS ROG

In short: the TUF A16 is a tough, affordable laptop for 1080p gaming. The Strix SCAR 18 is one of the fastest and most expensive laptops you can buy.

Read the details on ASUS ROG

ASUS sells gaming laptops under its ROG brand, which stands for Republic of Gamers. Inside ROG there are two lines that matter most. TUF is the affordable, tough line. Strix is the higher-end line, and it includes the top model, called SCAR.

ASUS TUF Gaming A16 (2026)

From about $1,100 to $1,700, depending on the GPU

This is ASUS's budget gaming laptop, and it is a strong one. It uses an AMD Ryzen processor and comes with a choice of RTX 5050, 5060 or 5070 graphics. The screen is 16 inches, and most versions run at 165Hz, which is smooth for fast games.

ASUS builds the TUF line to military toughness standards, meaning it is tested against drops, dust and extreme temperatures. The keyboard has RGB lighting, and the cooling system uses two fans with dust filters that you can clean.

ASUS ROG Strix SCAR 18 (2026)

Around $5,500 for the RTX 5090 model, US list price

This is ASUS's flagship, and it is built to be the fastest laptop ASUS makes, not the most portable one. It has an 18-inch screen with Mini LED backlighting. That means it can show much brighter highlights and deeper black areas than a normal screen. The screen runs at up to 240Hz at 4K resolution, though it needs to be switched into a special performance mode to reach that speed.

Inside is Intel's strongest laptop processor and an RTX 5090 with 24GB of memory. The whole laptop is allowed to use up to 320 watts across the chip and GPU together. That is a lot of power for a laptop. It also has a tool-free bottom panel, so you can open it and add memory or storage yourself without any screws.

It weighs close to 3.7 kilograms, so this is not a laptop you carry around all day. It is meant to sit on a desk most of the time.

05

MSI

In short: the Vector 16 is a well-built mid-range laptop with fast connections. The Titan 18 is MSI's no-compromise flagship, with a mechanical keyboard and huge cooling.

Read the details on MSI

MSI's gaming laptops sit in a few lines. Vector covers the mid-range. Raider sits just above it. Titan is the flagship line, built with almost no limits on size, power or price.

MSI Vector 16 HX AI

From about $1,500 to $2,800, depending on the GPU

The Vector 16 comes with a wide range of GPUs, from RTX 5070 up to RTX 5080, so the price changes a lot depending on which one you pick. It has a 16-inch screen, and higher configurations offer a sharper 2560x1600 panel at up to 240Hz.

It includes Thunderbolt 5 ports, the fastest connection currently available on a laptop. That is useful for an external monitor or a fast external drive. MSI calls its power boost feature OverBoost, which lets the CPU and GPU share extra wattage when a game needs it.

MSI Titan 18 HX AI

Around $5,700 to $6,000 for the RTX 5090 model, US list price

The Titan 18 is built to be the biggest, most complete gaming laptop MSI sells. It has an 18-inch Mini LED screen at up to 3840x2400 resolution, one of the sharpest available on any laptop.

MSI gives it a mechanical keyboard, the same kind of switches found in high-end desktop keyboards, rather than the flatter keys most laptops use. It also has a six-speaker sound system made with Dynaudio, a proper audio brand, instead of the basic speakers most laptops rely on.

Cooling is a full vapor chamber system, a flat sealed chamber that spreads heat more evenly than simple heat pipes. The laptop can draw up to 270 watts across the chip and GPU. It weighs about 3.6 kilograms, so like the SCAR 18, it is built for a desk, not a bag.

06

Lenovo Legion

In short: the Legion Pro 5i is good value for 1440p gaming. The Legion Pro 7i is often called the best all-round flagship, because of its strong cooling and its OLED screen.

Read the details on Lenovo Legion

Lenovo's Legion line has built a reputation for good cooling and a calmer design than some rivals, without giving up speed. The line has two main tiers worth knowing. Legion Pro 5i is for mid-range buyers. Legion Pro 7i is for people who want the most power Lenovo offers.

Lenovo Legion Pro 5i

Starts at about $1,500

The Pro 5i pairs Intel's Core Ultra 9 processor with graphics up to an RTX 5070 Ti, and up to 32GB of memory. It has a 16-inch OLED screen at up to 165Hz. That is a real advantage at this price. OLED screens usually show deeper black tones and more accurate colour than the LCD screens found in most laptops this cheap.

Cooling is rated for up to 200 watts, which is enough for the GPU options offered on this model, without pushing into flagship territory.

Lenovo Legion Pro 7i (Gen 10)

Starts at about $2,400 for the RTX 5080 model, more for RTX 5090

Several independent reviewers name the Legion Pro 7i as the strongest all-round gaming laptop this year. It balances speed, cooling and price better than most rivals. It has a 16-inch OLED screen at 2560x1600, running at up to 240Hz, and up to 64GB of memory.

Lenovo's cooling system here is called Legion Coldfront Vapor, and it is rated for up to 250 watts. Reviewers compared it to other RTX 5080 laptops. It consistently ran faster. The cooling let the chip use more of its rated power for longer, instead of slowing down after a few minutes.

At 2.57 kilograms, it is noticeably lighter than the ASUS and MSI flagships above, while still offering nearly flagship-level speed. That combination is the main reason reviewers like it so much.

Part 3What actually changes daily lifeScreen, heat, noise, battery and upgrades
08

Screens: size, speed and sharpness

In short: a 16-inch screen is easier to carry. An 18-inch screen is more immersive but heavier. OLED screens look best but drain the battery faster in bright scenes.

Read the details on screens

Screen size changes more than you might expect. A 16-inch laptop fits into more bags and feels lighter in your hands. An 18-inch laptop shows more detail and feels closer to a desktop monitor. But it adds real weight and takes up more desk space.

Refresh rate is how many times the screen redraws each second. For fast games like shooters, 165Hz or higher feels noticeably smoother than 60Hz. For slower story games, the difference matters less.

Screen technology matters too. OLED screens, used in most Lenovo Legion models and some others, show true black and very rich colour. LCD screens, including the Mini LED type used in the ASUS and MSI flagships, cannot go as dark in places. But Mini LED narrows that gap. It uses many small backlight zones instead of one big one. Mini LED screens can also get brighter overall, which helps in a sunny room.

One practical note: several of these screens only reach their fastest refresh rate in a specific performance mode, which uses more battery. If you see "up to 240Hz", check whether that is the normal setting or a special mode you have to turn on.

09

Cooling and noise

In short: a laptop that cools well stays fast for longer. All three brands use similar fan-and-heat-pipe designs, but the flagship models add a vapor chamber, which spreads heat better.

Read the details on cooling and noise

A gaming laptop can slow itself down on purpose, to avoid overheating. This is called thermal throttling. It happens more in thin, light laptops, because they have less room for fans and heat pipes.

A vapor chamber is a flat, sealed metal chamber filled with a small amount of liquid. As one part heats up, the liquid inside turns to vapor. It moves to a cooler part and turns back to liquid there. This spreads heat evenly across a wide area. It works better than simple heat pipes in laptops that push a lot of power. That is why MSI's Titan and Lenovo's Legion Pro 7i both use one.

Reviewers who tested the Legion Pro 7i said its cooling let the RTX 5080 chip run at close to full power. That held true for long gaming sessions. Some rival laptops with the same chip slowed down after only a few minutes. That is a real difference you would notice in a long gaming session, not just in a performance score chart.

Noise is the tradeoff. A laptop that cools well under full load is usually louder, because the fans work harder. All three brands let you choose a quieter mode for lighter tasks, which slows the fans and reduces the GPU's power a little.

10

Battery life

In short: do not expect long gaming battery life from any of these laptops. A few hours of light, unplugged work is normal. Plan to game with the charger plugged in.

Read the details on battery life

Gaming laptops are not built for long battery life while gaming, and none of the three brands here are an exception. A powerful GPU uses a lot of power, so most gaming laptops last only one to two hours on battery while playing a demanding game.

For lighter use, such as browsing or writing, battery life is much better. It is often six to eight hours. The laptop can switch off the dedicated GPU and use a smaller chip built into the processor instead.

Reviewers noted that Lenovo's high-end Legion Pro 7i has poor gaming battery life for its class. Its everyday battery life, away from games, is more reasonable. This is common at the flagship level. If battery life while gaming matters to you, a lower-powered GPU, like an RTX 5060 or 5070, will always last longer unplugged than an RTX 5090.

The simplest plan: treat a gaming laptop as a powerful desktop that happens to fold up. Keep the charger nearby whenever you plan to game.

11

Upgrading later, and keeping it working

In short: most of these laptops let you add more memory and storage later. The GPU cannot be changed. Keep the fans and vents clean.

Read the details on upgrades and care

On a gaming laptop, the GPU is built into the motherboard. You cannot swap it out later, unlike a desktop computer. So choose the GPU tier you need now, or a little above it, since it is the one part you cannot change afterward.

Memory and storage are a different story. Most of these laptops have memory slots and storage slots you can open later. That includes the ASUS TUF A16, the MSI Vector 16 and the Lenovo Legion Pro models. You or a technician can add more memory or storage when you need it. The ASUS Strix SCAR 18 even removed the screws from its bottom panel this year, so opening it is easier than before.

Whichever laptop you buy, plan to clean the fans and air vents every few months, especially if you live somewhere dusty. Dust buildup is one of the most common reasons a gaming laptop starts running hotter and louder over time. It is easy to prevent with a can of compressed air.

Part 4Making the choiceWho should buy which, and how to buy here
12

Who should buy which

Buy a budget model if

  • You mostly play 1080p or online games
  • You want to spend under $1,700
  • You need a laptop that also handles schoolwork or office tasks
  • You want the toughest, most drop-resistant build

Buy a flagship if

  • You want the newest games at 4K, maxed out
  • You edit video or do 3D work alongside gaming
  • The laptop will mostly live on a desk
  • You want the screen to be as good as the GPU

Four short cases

The student on a budget. The ASUS TUF A16 with an RTX 5060 or 5070 covers games and schoolwork, and its tough build survives a backpack well.

The competitive online gamer. Look for a high refresh rate over raw GPU power. An RTX 5070 with a 240Hz screen beats an RTX 5090 with a slower screen for fast-paced games.

The person who also edits video or works in 3D. An RTX 5080 laptop with plenty of memory, such as the Legion Pro 7i or the MSI Vector at its top configuration, balances cost and power well.

The buyer who wants the absolute best. Any of the three RTX 5090 flagships will satisfy you. Pick by weight and screen size preference, since the raw speed is very close between them.

13

Buying in Uzbekistan

Every figure above is a US price, so expect your local shop to differ. The checklist below covers the details worth confirming before you pay.

  • Get the warranty in writing. A laptop bought through an official ASUS, MSI or Lenovo channel usually qualifies for local repairs. An unofficial import might look cheaper at checkout, but repairs can cost far more later.
  • Check the power adapter. Flagship laptops draw a lot of power and need a large charger. Confirm it is made for our mains voltage, or bring a proper adapter.
  • Confirm the exact model number. The same name can be sold with different GPUs or screens in different countries. Match the model number on the box to the specification page, not just the name.
  • Plan for the weight. An 18-inch flagship is heavy to carry daily. If you will move it often, weigh that against the extra performance.
  • Budget for a cooling pad. A laptop cooling stand is cheap and can help in a warm room, especially in summer.
FAQ

Questions people ask

Is the RTX 5090 laptop worth the extra money over the RTX 5080?

Usually not, unless you truly need the extra power. Several reviewers found the gap between the two is smaller than the price gap, and recommend the RTX 5080 as the smarter buy for most people.

Which brand has the best cooling?

Reviewers currently rate Lenovo's Legion Pro line highly for cooling, especially the Legion Pro 7i. MSI's Titan and ASUS's SCAR both use strong cooling too, in larger, heavier bodies.

Can I use a gaming laptop for work as well?

Yes. All of these laptops run normal office and study software easily. The extra graphics power just sits unused until you open a game.

How long will a gaming laptop's battery last while gaming?

Usually one to two hours, regardless of brand. For longer unplugged use, expect to close demanding games and switch to lighter tasks.

Do I need an RTX 5090 if I only play older games?

No. An RTX 5060 or 5070 plays older and lighter games very well, and costs much less. Save the RTX 5090 for the newest, heaviest titles.
