Samsung or Artel: which refrigerator fits your kitchen?

A global brand with a wide range against a local brand that is close to home. The better choice depends less on the logo and more on your kitchen, your power supply and who will fix it.

By Abdulloh Mahmudov  |  Updated October 2026  |  About 15 minute read

Artel appliances are made for the local market.

Samsung

Pick it if

You want a wide choice of sizes and layouts, such as side-by-side or French-door models. It also fits if you value modern cooling features like inverter compressors and separate cooling zones, and you can find a model with good local service.

Artel

Pick it if

You want a sensible price, easy access to service and parts, and a refrigerator designed with local conditions in mind. It suits plain, reliable everyday use better than a showpiece kitchen.

A refrigerator runs twenty-four hours a day for ten years or more. It is probably the appliance in your home that you think about least and that would hurt the most if it failed in July. That is a good reason to spend an evening comparing options instead of buying whatever the salesperson points at.

This page compares Samsung and Artel refrigerators as two types of choice. We do not claim to have tested individual models in a lab, and both brands sell many different models that change often. So instead of arguing about one fridge against another, we explain what to compare, what usually differs between a global brand and a local one, and how to check the details on the exact model in front of you. The specific figures you need, such as capacity, energy use and warranty, are always printed on the label or the maker's page for that model.

Who these two brands are

Samsung is a South Korean company that sells refrigerators in almost every country. Its range is wide. You can find narrow two-door models for small apartments, large side-by-side units with water dispensers, and French-door designs with wide fresh-food sections. Many of its fridges are sold with digital inverter compressors, and some higher models have app support through a Wi-Fi connection.

Artel is a home-appliance brand from Uzbekistan. It makes and sells televisions, refrigerators, air conditioners and other household appliances for the local market. Its refrigerator range is narrower than Samsung's and is aimed at ordinary family kitchens, with plain layouts and familiar sizes. The main selling points tend to be price, local availability and easier service, which matter more than they sound.

Because the ranges differ, a fair comparison is not "which brand is better." It is "which model from each brand fits my kitchen, and which one will be easier to live with when something needs attention."

Measure before you fall in love with a model

The best refrigerator on paper is useless if it does not fit through your door. Measure these things before you go to the shop, and write them down.

  • Width, height and depth of the space where the fridge will stand.
  • Door and corridor width along the delivery path, including turns on the stairs and the lift.
  • Clearance for the door to open. A fridge door that hits a wall or a counter and only opens halfway is a daily annoyance.
  • Ventilation space. Makers usually ask for a few centimetres at the back, the sides and the top, so the heat can escape. A fridge pushed tight into a cupboard works harder and wears faster.

Standard-width models from both brands are close to 60 centimetres wide, though there are narrower and much wider options too, and a few millimetres' difference rarely matters. Where the two brands often differ is height, depth and door swing, so compare the exact dimensions on each model's label, not the picture in the catalogue.

A simple trick: cut a piece of cardboard to the fridge's width and depth and put it on the floor where the fridge will stand. Open a cupboard door next to it and walk past it. You will find problems in a minute that a tape measure misses.

Cooling types: what the words mean

No Frost or drip defrost

A No Frost refrigerator blows cold air around the inside with a fan and melts frost automatically, so you never defrost it by hand. A drip, or static, fridge cools through a cold back wall and needs occasional defrosting. No Frost is more convenient and keeps temperatures more even. Drip models are simpler, often cheaper and a little quieter, and the fresh section can feel slightly more humid, which some people like for vegetables. Samsung sells many No Frost models. Artel sells both kinds depending on the range, so check the exact model.

Inverter compressor

A normal compressor switches fully on and off. An inverter compressor adjusts its speed up and down, which usually means quieter running, steadier temperatures and lower energy use. Samsung promotes its inverter technology and often backs the compressor with a long separate warranty in many countries. Check whether the model you are looking at has an inverter and how long the compressor warranty lasts where you live. Some Artel models also use inverter compressors, while others use a conventional one, so again, look at the label.

Separate cooling for fridge and freezer

Some higher-end Samsung models use separate evaporators for the fridge and freezer. This keeps the fridge section from drying out and stops food smells moving between sections. It is a real benefit, though you will pay for it. In a normal family kitchen, a single-circuit No Frost fridge is fine, as long as you cover strong-smelling food.

Refrigerant

Almost all modern fridges use R600a, which is isobutane. It is efficient and has a low climate impact. It is flammable in large amounts, but the quantity in a home fridge is very small. It is one more reason to avoid fixing a cooling circuit yourself.

Climate class and hot summers

This is the part that people most often miss, and it matters in a country with summers as hot as ours. Every refrigerator has a climate class printed on the label, such as SN, N, ST or T. It tells you the room temperature range the fridge is designed for.

ClassDesigned for rooms aroundComment
SN10 to 32 °CFine for a cool, climate-controlled room, not ideal for a hot kitchen
N16 to 32 °CNormal for temperate climates
ST16 to 38 °CBetter for warm kitchens
T16 to 43 °CBest for very hot rooms and summer heat

If your kitchen reaches 35 degrees in July, a fridge rated only for 32 will struggle. The compressor runs almost constantly, the inside may not reach the right temperature, and the machine wears faster. Look for ST or T if you cook with the windows shut or the kitchen faces the sun. A global brand may sell the same model in different classes in different regions, so make sure the class printed on the sticker of the unit you are buying is the right one. Local brands usually design for the local climate by default, but you should still read the label.

Power cuts and voltage swings

Voltage can wobble in many homes, especially in the evening in summer when everybody runs air conditioners. Brief cuts happen too. A fridge deals with this better than most devices, but there are things to watch.

  • Restart delay. If power returns and the compressor restarts immediately, the pressure in the system has not balanced and the compressor can be damaged. Good fridges include protection that waits a few minutes. Cheaper units rely on the user.
  • Wide voltage tolerance. Some models state the voltage range they accept. A wider range is better in a place where voltage swings.
  • Inverter models tend to start more gently, but the electronics inside can be sensitive to surges, so a good surge protector with a time delay is worth having.

Plug the fridge directly into the wall, or into a protector that is meant for fridges. A cheap extension cord is not a good idea. If a cut lasts several hours, keep the doors shut. A closed fridge keeps food safe for about four hours, and a full freezer for a day or so if it is not opened.

Capacity and layout

Capacity is quoted in litres, but there are two numbers that look alike. Gross capacity is the total volume of the box. Net or usable capacity is the space you can actually use for food after the shelves, drawers and frames. When you compare two fridges, make sure you compare the same number. A fridge with a slightly smaller usable capacity can still feel bigger if the shelves are better arranged.

Think about how your household eats, not about the biggest number.

  • Do you freeze a lot? Bread, meat and vegetables for the winter need a bigger freezer. A bottom-freezer layout gives you more drawers and a bigger fresh section. A top-freezer layout is cheaper and simpler.
  • Do you store tall items? Large pans, watermelons and bottles need shelf height. Look for shelves that move.
  • Do you buy in bulk? A large door bin and a deep vegetable drawer matter more than a smart display.
  • How many people share it? A family of five and a couple living alone need very different interiors.

Samsung offers more variety in layout, including larger side-by-side models that are far wider than a standard unit. Artel's range is more traditional, with the familiar tall two-door shape that fits most kitchens without surprises. If you have a narrow kitchen, the familiar shape is not a weakness.

Noise

Noise is quoted in decibels, written as dB(A). Many fridges sit between about 35 and 42 dB(A). Lower numbers are quieter, and each three-decibel step is a noticeable difference. Inverter models tend to run quieter because the compressor slows down instead of switching hard on and off. In an open-plan flat or a studio, noise matters a lot, and you will hear a loud compressor at night. In a closed kitchen on the other side of the house, you may never notice.

The number on the label is measured in a quiet lab. At home, a fridge can sound louder if it stands on an uneven floor, touches a wall or has loose items on top. Level the feet, leave a gap from the wall and keep the top clear.

Energy use and running cost

Every fridge has an energy rating and an annual consumption figure in kilowatt-hours. These can look different on labels depending on the system used, so compare the kWh per year first, because that is a straight number. A typical modern family fridge might use somewhere between 200 and 450 kWh per year, depending on size, age and type. Multiply by your local price per kWh to see the yearly cost.

It is worth doing the math. A fridge that costs a little more but uses 100 kWh less per year can pay back the difference over its life. Over a ten-year life, a small difference in yearly use adds up. But do not chase the lowest consumption blindly, because a bigger fridge naturally uses more than a small one. Compare fridges of a similar capacity and class.

Real consumption also depends on the room temperature, how often you open the door and how full the fridge is. A full fridge is more efficient than an empty one, because the cold food helps hold the temperature. Warm food placed inside, a door left open or a fridge next to the oven all cost you.

Service, parts and warranty

This is where local and global brands feel the most different, and it deserves more weight than the list of features. A fridge that cannot be repaired quickly is a fridge you replace early.

What to ask

  • How long is the warranty for the whole unit, and is the compressor covered for longer?
  • Is the warranty honoured by the shop, the importer or the maker, and where is the nearest service centre in your city?
  • How long does a technician usually take to arrive?
  • Are spare parts such as door seals, fans, thermostats and shelves available in the country, and what do they cost?
  • Does the warranty stay valid if you move the fridge to another address?

A local brand often has a short chain between the factory and the repair shop, and a technician who knows its models, which is a real advantage when a part fails. A global brand often has a longer warranty on the compressor and a larger network, but parts for some models may need to be ordered from abroad, which can take time. The truth is different in different cities, so call the service centre before you buy, and ask the questions above. If an answer is vague, that tells you something.

Keep your receipt and the warranty card, and photograph the serial number sticker inside the fridge on the day it arrives.

Price and value

As a general pattern, a Samsung refrigerator costs more than an Artel refrigerator of a similar size, and the gap grows with features like inverter compressors, door-in-door designs, water dispensers and app control. We will not quote exact prices, because they change from month to month and between shops. But keep these points in mind when you compare.

  • Compare similar models. A basic Artel against a premium Samsung is not a fair test. Compare the same capacity, the same cooling type and the same climate class.
  • Look at the full cost. Delivery, installation, a surge protector and an extended warranty can change the real price.
  • Check instalment terms. Instalment plans are common, but interest and fees vary. Compare the total you will pay with the cash price.
  • Do not pay for features you will not use. A display and a Wi-Fi app are fun, but a good door seal is worth more.

The best value is often a mid-range model from either brand with the right size, a No Frost system, the right climate class and a good warranty. The top of the range is for people who want a specific layout or a design statement, not for people who want cold milk.

Which one for whom

A small flat or a rental

A compact two-door model, either brand. Prioritise width, noise and a simple layout, and pick the one with the better local service and the better price.

A family of four to six

A bottom-freezer No Frost model with a large usable capacity. Both brands have options. Check the freezer drawers, because families who stock up on food use them heavily.

A hot kitchen without much ventilation

Check for climate class ST or T before anything else, and leave the ventilation gap. A model with an inverter compressor will handle the heat better.

A large, modern kitchen

Samsung's side-by-side and French-door models are designed for this, and few local ranges match their variety. Just check that the doors and the delivery path allow it.

Someone who cares most about easy repairs

Choose the brand with the closest service centre and the clearest warranty terms in your city. This can be either brand, and the answer is on the phone, not in the brochure.

A tight budget

A simple model from either brand. Do not skip the climate class and the warranty to save a small amount of money.

What to check in the shop

  • Read the sticker for climate class, capacity, kWh per year and noise.
  • Open and close the doors several times. The seal should grip evenly with no gaps, and the door should close on its own from a small angle.
  • Pull out drawers and move the shelves. Cheap plastic that feels thin or cracks easily is a bad sign.
  • Look inside for a bright, even light and a clear temperature control.
  • Ask whether the display model is the same as the box you will get.
  • Ask when the unit was made. A fridge that has sat in a warehouse for two years is not new in every sense.
  • Check the packaging for damage before you accept the delivery, and open the box in front of the courier.

Setup and care

When the fridge arrives, let it stand upright for a few hours before you plug it in, particularly if it was transported on its side. The oil in the compressor needs time to settle. Then run it empty for a few hours to cool down before you load it.

  • Set the fridge to around 4 °C and the freezer to around minus 18 °C. Colder than that wastes power.
  • Clean the door seals every month with warm water, and check that they are not cracked.
  • Vacuum or brush the dust off the back or bottom grille two or three times a year, if your model has an accessible one.
  • Do not overfill a No Frost fridge so that the vents are blocked, because the air needs to move.
  • Let hot food cool before you put it in.
  • In a long absence, empty it, switch it off, clean it and leave the door slightly open so it does not get musty.

Questions people ask

Is Samsung always better than Artel?

No. Samsung has more variety and more advanced features, but a plain, well-chosen Artel can do the daily job just as well for less money. The better choice is the one that fits your kitchen, your climate and your service situation.

Do I need a voltage stabiliser for a fridge?

Not always. Many modern fridges tolerate normal swings, and a surge protector with a time delay is often enough. If your voltage is very unstable, check the voltage range in the manual and ask a technician. Use a stabiliser that is designed for the power of a refrigerator.

Is No Frost worth the extra money?

For most families, yes, because you never have to defrost it and the temperature stays even. If you want the simplest, cheapest option and don't mind defrosting twice a year, a drip model is fine.

What is the best climate class for Uzbekistan?

ST or T is safest for hot summers, especially in kitchens without air conditioning. Always check the sticker on the unit, not only the catalogue.

How long should a refrigerator last?

Ten to fifteen years is common for a well-kept unit. The compressor, door seals and electronic board are the parts that most often fail, so ask about their warranty and spare parts.

Can I put the fridge next to the oven or the radiator?

It is better not to. Heat from nearby appliances makes the fridge work harder and use more power. Leave a gap or fit a heat barrier if there is no other place.

Should I buy an extended warranty?

Read what it covers. If it only repeats what the standard warranty already gives, it is not worth it. If it covers the compressor and the electronics for a longer period at a fair price, it can be useful for an expensive model.

Is it worth buying an older model on sale?

Often yes. Refrigerators change slowly, and last year's model can be a bargain. Just make sure it is new, has a valid warranty and still has parts available.

The short checklist

  • Measure the space, the door swing and the delivery path.
  • Choose No Frost or drip, and decide if you want an inverter.
  • Check the climate class for your kitchen.
  • Compare kWh per year and noise for similar sizes.
  • Call the service centre and ask about warranty and parts.
  • Compare the total price, including delivery and instalment costs.
